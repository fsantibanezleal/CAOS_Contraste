# Case C02, corporate PD across the 1 to 5 year horizons on the UCI Polish bankruptcy files

- **Category:** `credit-scoring`
- **Source:** real outcomes (`real-outcomes`): the UCI Polish Companies Bankruptcy files, five ARFF members of
  `polish+companies+bankruptcy+data.zip` (source `uci-polish`, mirror-allowed, CC BY 4.0, DOI 10.24432/C5F600), read from the device data
  root. Methods: `riskvalidation` (discrimination, calibration, stability, `ml`, `validation.ml`, `provisions`, `validation.ifrs9`; engine
  contracts `engine-ml-api` and `engine-provisions-api`, pinned by the commit in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`). Code:
  `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c02/`; documents: `data/derived/C02/`; contracts `c02-outputs` and `c02-models` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** how fast discrimination falls from the 1-year to the 5-year horizon, whether the learned rungs beat the scorecard by a
  margin a paired test supports, and whether they stay calibrated. The files are undated: no out-of-time claim.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of credit risk of a corporate or SME book, with the model validator, owns the question of how far ahead a PD model
can see. A provision under IFRS 9 needs a PD for the next 12 months and, once credit risk has risen, for the whole life of the exposure
(IFRS 9 5.5.17 to 5.5.20); the supervisory report on lifetime PD back-testing (EBA/Rep/2023/36, paragraphs 114 to 118) asks for the
observed cumulative default against the predicted one by horizon. The reason to act now is that a model validated on one horizon is
silently reused on the others, and the gain a challenger promises on one file is not the gain on another.

**Outcomes.**

| Outcome | Proof point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The scorecard's discrimination depends strongly on the file | the P1 scorecard's holdout AUC is 0.728 on the 1year file and 0.911 on the 5year file, and the figures for 2year, 3year and 4year are 0.792, 0.839 and 0.810; read with the file name as the horizon, discrimination rises; read as the UCI page describes the files (the 1year file has the longest horizon, 5 years), it falls from the shortest to the longest horizon | `outputs.rungs[].discrimination` of `data/derived/C02/h*.json` |
| A learned rung beats the scorecard by a margin a paired test supports | in every one of the six documents the paired DeLong test supports at least one learned rung over P1; on the 1year file LightGBM reaches 0.943 against 0.728 | `outputs.challenger` |
| The gain comes with overfit and calibration flags the validator must read | the train minus holdout AUC gap exceeds the 0.05 convention for 6 of the 7 rungs on the 1year and 2year files; the Hosmer-Lemeshow test rejects (p below 0.01) for several rungs in every document | `outputs.rungs[].overfit`, `tests` |
| The transfer across files is weak and cannot be trusted | a scorecard fitted on the 1year file scores 0.644 to 0.829 on the other holdouts against 0.728 on its own (exploratory: the firm overlap is unknown) | `outputs.transfer` |

**Positioning.** The case does not say which horizon a file belongs to without the open decision below, and it does not certify a model. It
reproduces on public data the tests a validator would run, with the engine of Contraste, and shows how far each answer moves with the
rung and the file.

**Credibility.** The rows are the UCI files (7,027, 10,173, 10,503, 9,792 and 5,910 rows with 271, 400, 495, 515 and 410 bankruptcies); the
reader refuses a file whose counts differ. Every statistic is computed by `riskvalidation`, binning, imputation and selection are fitted on
the training slice only, the calibration map on a separate slice, and the documents pass the schema and `check_c02` before they are
written. The unknowns are named: the firm overlap across the files, the horizon mapping and the absence of a date.

## Why this case exists

A validator is asked two questions of a corporate PD model: does it still rank firms well at a longer horizon, and is the extra
complexity of a learned model worth the validation burden? The five files are the only open corporate dataset in the product with a
bankruptcy label at several horizons, so the case is where a horizon-by-horizon comparison of the whole C01 ladder can be made on real
outcomes, and where the limits of such a comparison (no firm identifier, no date) are most visible.

## Data and what is real

The raw input is `polish+companies+bankruptcy+data.zip` in the device data root (`raw/uci-polish/`, never the repository), pinned by its
SHA-256 in `data/sources/manifest.json`; the bake fetches it from the UCI landing when it is absent and refuses other bytes. The rows of the
committed copy are in `data/derived/C02/raw-sources.txt`.

What is real: the 64 financial ratios and the bankruptcy label of each statement. What is a convention, labelled so in the documents:

- the horizon of a document is the name of its file (decision Q9 and the contracts). The UCI page describes the file named kyear as the
  ratios of the k-th year of the forecasting period with the class label "bankruptcy status after" 6 - k years (the 1year file after 5
  years). This is UNVERIFIED against the paper and is an open decision for the main session (finding `F-HORIZON-MAPPING` in every document);
- contract 1 needs an identity and a date the files do not carry: `id` is `<horizon>-<row>` and the observation date is 2013-12-31, the end
  of the span the UCI page gives (2000 to 2013); no temporal claim rests on it;
- the pooled variant is the five files stacked with a horizon indicator, its slices the union of the five files' slices; the firm overlap
  across the files is unknown (UNVERIFIED; no feature vector is shared by two files, which is not evidence that firms do not repeat);
- the missing ratios (`?`) are imputed with the training median and ratios missing in 5 percent or more of the training slice also get a
  0/1 indicator; the overfit tolerance 0.05 AUC points (decision V6); 1,000 resamples of the paired Brier bootstrap; a two-notch grade
  disagreement; a loss given default of 45 percent in the Impact block (an assumption).

## Formalization

Per file and for the pooled variant, with a locked stratified holdout of 30 percent, a calibration slice of 20 percent of the rest, the
training slice and repeated stratified folds inside it:

1. the C01 ladder on the training slice (P0 constant and single variable, P1 WoE scorecard, P2a L1, P2b PLTR, P3 EBM, P4 LightGBM and
   XGBoost; P5 TabPFN is not run), calibration maps for the learned rungs on the calibration slice, no resampling;
2. per rung on the holdout: AUC with the DeLong interval, KS, Hosmer-Lemeshow, Brier, ECE with the reliability diagram, the Jeffreys and
   binomial level tests, the PSI of the PD and the CSI of each input against the training slice, the overfit gap;
3. each learned rung against the P1 scorecard: the DeLong difference of the AUC, the paired Brier bootstrap and the share of obligors whose
   grade differs by two notches or more;
4. exploratory blocks: the transfer (a rung fitted on a file, scored on the holdout of every file), the five event rates as a cumulative
   PD through the engine's term structure with the lifetime back-test row, and the 12-month and lifetime ECL of one unit of exposure under
   those PDs through `engine-provisions-api`.

The constant PD is fitted and recorded in the models document and has no battery: a constant has no risk groups, so the Hosmer-Lemeshow
test is undefined for it. Descriptive figures (AUC, KS, Brier, ECE) are the rung's entry in `outputs`; the `tests` rows carry only rows with
a light or a p-value, because the contract refuses a `not_evaluated` row that carries a number.

## Expected results

Declared before the bake and wide on purpose: the bankruptcy rate of a file lies between 1 and 30 percent, the holdout AUC of the scorecard
and of LightGBM between 0.55 and 1.0, and the train minus holdout gap of the scorecard between -0.10 and 0.45. A result outside is a finding
to read, not a range to tune.

## Reading the results

Discrimination differs far more between files than between the scorecard and the learned rungs on the later files, and the order of the
files is the file name's, not a verified horizon: the AUC rises with the file number, which is a decay in discrimination only under the
UCI page's mapping. The decision is open and the case does not take it. A learned rung that beats the scorecard by a significant margin is
a finding, because the champion is not the best rung, but the same rung usually shows the larger overfit gap; a validator weighs both. The
transfer and the pooled results are exploratory: repeated firms would flatter them.

## Validation anchor

Every number of a document equals a `riskvalidation` call on the arrays the document was computed from (`tests/c02/test_c02_battery.py`
recomputes them from the planted files). The documents pass `tests/contracts_u14/semantics.py::check_c02`.

## Limits

- No date and no firm identifier: no out-of-time validation, and the firm overlap across the files is unknown.
- The horizon mapping of the files is an open decision (see Data and what is real).
- The holdout is a random stratified one; 60 to 90 statements per file repeat exactly an earlier row of the same file and are kept.
- The champion's mean PD on the five holdouts is not strictly increasing, so the Impact block uses the file event rates of the
  term-structure block; the lifetime back-test row compares the champion's holdout mean PD with the holdout event rate.
- The loss given default (45 percent) and the absence of discounting are assumptions of an illustration, not a provision.
- Calibration maps for P3 and P4 are isotonic and fitted on a calibration slice of a few hundred bankruptcies at most.
