# Case C04, rating transitions and TTC PD by grade

- **Category:** ratings and calibration
- **Sources:** ESMA's central repository of rating statistics, CEREP (`esma-cerep`, **mirror-allowed**: "Reproduction
  of all information on this site (REGISTERS information) is authorised except as otherwise stated, provided the source
  is acknowledged"; every table derived from it says "Source: ESMA CEREP; tables transformed by Contraste"): the
  corporate long-term statistics of S&P's, Moody's and Fitch's EU entities, calendar-year cohorts 2000 to 2025 and
  semester cohorts 2010 to 2025. Three papers, **derived-only** (fetched into the device data root and parsed there;
  the agency matrices they reprint never enter an artifact): Israel, Rosenthal and Wei (2001), *Finding generators for
  Markov chains via empirical transition matrices, with applications to credit ratings*, Mathematical Finance 11(2),
  245 to 265, DOI 10.1111/1467-9965.00114; Schuermann and Hanson (2004), *Estimating probabilities of default*, FRBNY
  Staff Report 190; Engelmann (2024), *Spurious default probability projections in credit risk stress testing models*,
  arXiv 2401.08892. The known-truth variants are drawn from `riskvalidation` 0.4.0's `RatingPaths` with the EM generator
  of S&P's pooled counts (truth status `synthetic-known-truth`).
- **One line:** what the agencies' published migrations say about PDs by grade, and how far a migration matrix can be
  trusted to give them, to project them, or to show that the chain behind it is Markov and stable.

## Why this case exists

A validator meets migration matrices wherever a PD must hold beyond one year or across a cycle: a through-the-cycle
grade's PD may be estimated with "migration matrices for the purpose of estimating long-run average default rates
covering the full historical observation period" (EBA/GL/2017/16, Table 1, "TTC E"); the rating philosophy is analysed
through "migration across grades or pools" and "changes in the yearly default rates of each grade or pool" (paragraph
67); IFRS 9 lifetime PDs and stress-test projections chain one-year matrices. Each use rests on choices a model owner
seldom shows: which default definition, what happens to ratings withdrawn during the year, which estimator, whether the
chain is Markov and the same every year. The agencies publish their migrations through ESMA, so the choices can be
measured on real data; and on a generator whose truth is known, their consequences can be measured exactly.

## Formalisation

### The data: what CEREP counts

CEREP publishes aggregates only, built on "a stock concept model, so that intra-period rating activity is derived from
a comparison of ratings at the beginning and the end of a period" (CEREP help file, ESMA65-8-10634). Three of its pages
count defaults, each differently (help file, sections 4.2 to 4.4, read verbatim):

| Definition | Page | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| D2 | default rates (tab 2) | "The distinct number of ratings included in the 'number of defaults' ... divided by the total number of ratings belonging in the initial cohort ... (regardless of whether they include a default or not)" |
| D3 | defaults by category (tab 3) | every default event of the beginning cohort, rows by "the category or notch of the rating at the BOP", columns by "the category or notch of the rating at the placement of the default event"; "all default events will be counted" |
| D4 | transitions (tab 4) | the ratings in a default category at the end of the period over the cohort less its withdrawals: "If the rating is withdrawn at the end of the statistics period, it is included in the 'Withdrawals' column according to the Category or Notch it was rated with at the start" |

"For the purposes of reporting into the CEREP, no deterministic definition of a default event has been set up.
Therefore, the definitions might differ for various CRAs". Moody's transition page has no default category at all
(its labels run from Aaa to C and WR), so its D4 does not exist.

The agencies' labels are mapped to one scale of seven performing grades (AAA, AA, A, BBB, BB, B, CCC to C, with Moody's
equivalents), default (S&P's R, SD and D; Fitch's RD and D) and withdrawals (the Withdrawals column with WD, WR and
NR; Fitch's DDD, DD and D before its 2006 scale change). Measured on the full fetch: tab 2's distinct defaulted
ratings equal tab 3's events grade by grade in every cohort of all three agencies, so D2 and D3 differ only in their
cohorts; tab 2's printed rates imply a larger cohort than tab 4's rows in 18 of S&P's 26 years (up to 19%: BB 2020,
1,340 against 1,129), 7 of Moody's 26 and 9 of Fitch's 24, never a smaller one, and ESMA states no reason. D2 is taken
over tab 2's own cohort and every gap is shown. Fitch's transition pages from 2006 to 2014 have default categories
that hold no rating at all while its default page counts 189 defaulted ratings in those years: they sit in the
withdrawals column.

### The fetch

The interface's JSON endpoints (`filters`, `searchStatistics/{tab}`) are called by POST with the body the interface
itself sends; 361 requests (the filters answer and, for each agency, tabs 2, 3 and 4 of every calendar year 2000 to
2025, tab 4 of every semester 2010 to 2025, and tabs 2 and 4 of the five five-year windows 2000 to 2024), at least
4.5 s apart, each answer cached in the data root and pinned in the licence manifest (`data-pipeline/fetch.py
esma-cerep`). The service answered in 16 to 65 s per request.

Read: a PD read from CEREP's transition matrix (D4) understates the speculative grades several times over: S&P's
default column gives 29% of the default page's CCC to C rate and 45% of its B rate over the years both pages hold,
Fitch's 19% and 25% (its 2006 to 2014 default columns are empty). Moody's has no D4 at all. The IRB risk weight of
S&P's cohort mix (F-IRB LGD 45%, maturity 2.5 years) is 58.0% with D2's PDs and 47.5% with D4's. The definition is
part of the PD.

**The chain behind the matrix.** The one-year matrix is not the same every year (time homogeneity: chi-square 12,028
on 1,075 degrees of freedom for S&P, 5,834 on 900 for Moody's, 3,888 on 782 for Fitch, p below 1e-300), and every one
of S&P's 26 years departs from the pooled matrix at 1%. No generator reproduces any agency's pooled matrix (Theorem
3(c): 6 moves reachable but never observed for S&P and Moody's, 15 for Fitch), none is stochastically monotone; the
diagonal and weighted adjustments come within L1 0.0013 to 0.0100 of the matrix, JLT 10 to 53 times further. AAA's
cohort PD is zero in every year of S&P and Moody's and in 23 of Fitch's 24; the EM generator gives S&P's AAA 0.66bp.

**The lifetime check.** Chaining one-year matrices understates what the five-year cohorts lived. S&P's CCC to C
cohort of 2020: 61.4% of its ratings defaulted within five years (the default page's window); the window's own annual
matrices chained give 9.1% and the pooled matrix to the fifth power 25.3%, and the window's transition page shows
none of them in default at its end (defaulted ratings are withdrawn). In 2015 to 2019: 46.5% lived, 14.8% chained.
Fitch's 2020 window: 54.2% lived, 9.5% chained; its 2010 to 2014 chain gives 0.0% (the empty default columns) where
22.4% lived.

**Known truth.** On rating paths from the EM generator of S&P's counts (200 repetitions per rung, five annual
snapshots, cohorts like S&P's mean cohort):

- *Estimators.* From annual snapshots, EM, the diagonal and weighted adjustments, the cohort and JLT miss AAA's
  0.66bp by 3.7bp to 3.8bp in RMSE, their biases within their Monte Carlo errors of one another; the duration
  estimator, which needs transition times, reaches 0.82bp. The cohort PD of AAA is exactly zero in 99% of
  repetitions, of AA in 83%.
- *Intervals.* The Wald interval covers AAA's true PD in 1% of repetitions and AA's in 17% (it collapses at no
  default); Agresti-Coull and Jeffreys hold near 95% or above in every grade; the duration PD's bootstrap covers AAA
  in 59% and AA in 28%.
- *Tests.* Time homogeneity, the reference test and the momentum hazard hold their size; the order test's default
  chi-square does not on these cohorts (10.0% at 5% over 200 repetitions; 7.10% and 2.25% at 5% and 1% over 2,000),
  while its likelihood ratio does (4.55%, 0.80%).
- *Momentum.* At alpha 0.025, 0.05, 0.125 and 0.25 the fitted hazard coefficient is 0.077, 0.145, 0.339 and 0.607;
  the hazard test sees the empirical strength (0.125) in 100% of repetitions, time homogeneity in 8.5%. The BBB
  cohort's five-year default frequency rises from 0.63% to 1.55%; the one-year matrix to the fifth power gives 1.48%,
  0.07 points short (4 Monte Carlo errors).
- *A recession year.* Downgrades times 1.25 for one year are seen by time homogeneity in 29.5% of repetitions, times
  1.5 in 98%; times 2, BB's PD that year is 2.3 times the stable chain's and the five-year long-run average 1.26
  times.
- *Informative withdrawals.* When withdrawal rises tenfold in the year before default (k 9), removing withdrawals
  understates B's PD by 34%, keeping them in the denominator by 38%, following them (EBA paragraph 76) by 15%; without
  informativeness removing them overstates it by 2.4%, keeping them understates it by 3.6% and following them is
  unbiased. Neither matrix test sees it.
- *Thin cohorts.* With 50 obligors per grade the Wald interval covers AAA's PD 0.33% of the time (Agresti-Coull 100%,
  Jeffreys 99.7%). Even at 1,000 obligors per grade the Jeffreys intervals of AAA and AA, and of A and BBB, overlap
  with probability 1.00; B and CCC to C separate from 200 obligors (overlap 0.21).

**Published answers.** Israel, Rosenthal and Wei: 8 of 9 distances to the printed six digits (the ninth only from
the printed generator); Schuermann and Hanson's Table 5: all twelve bounds and lengths with 15 defaults of 531, N
dagger 531, 84.3 and 45.8; Engelmann section 4: the TTC portfolio and its PD (1.198%), each starting PD and extreme.

## The anchored withdrawal bounds

The informative-withdrawals result above is the gap this section works on. Removing a withdrawn rating, keeping it in the
denominator or following it to the year end each answers a question the transition page cannot answer: would the withdrawn
rating have defaulted? The method is the engine's `riskvalidation.transitions.withdrawals` (release 1.1.0), set beside the
three CEREP treatments and the latent-chain EM generator. It states the PD of a grade as
a function of the odds ratio `k` of withdrawal for ratings that default within the period, PD(k) = (D + X(k)) / N with
X(k) = W k D / (k D + S): `k` = 1 is the usual estimate with withdrawals removed, `k` = 0 keeps them in the denominator, `k` to
infinity is Manski's (1989) bound. Where a second source counts the defaults of the same grade differently, `k` is read off it
(the anchor): CEREP's default page (tab 2) counts a rating that defaulted within the year whether or not it was withdrawn, the
transition page (tab 4) does not. The anchored interval inverts the anchor's Jeffreys interval through PD(k).

The study runs on the withdrawals family's own generator (the EM generator of S&P's counts, withdrawal at 6% a year and 6%
(1 + k) in the year before default), 200 repetitions per design and per `k` of 0, 1, 3 and 9, on draws of its own (seed keys
`withdrawals-bounds:...`, so the family's other numbers do not move). Four designs: the first-year cohort (the model's exact
setting), Contraste's five annual cohorts pooled (a rating withdrawn in an earlier year is missing from a later cohort, a selection
the one-period model does not see), a second source that counts 70% of the true defaults (a definition mismatch) and the
first-year cohort ten times larger. The criteria were fixed before the run (`docs/design/SDD.md`): the anchored
interval covers the true PD of B in at least 90% of the repetitions at `k` 1, 3 and 9; the existing treatments cover it in under
50% at `k` 9; the anchored `k` of the real cohorts is stable when at least 80% of the cohorts' bands contain the pooled `k`. The
producer is `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c04_bounds.py`; it calls the engine and counts. The tables are generated from the
committed documents (`data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c04_page.py`).

**Verdict of the plan's hypothesis: partly holds, partly refuted.** Coverage holds: the anchored interval covers the true
one-year PD of B (1.17%) in 97.5% to 100% of the 200 repetitions at every `k` in the first-year, pooled and ten-fold designs, and
covers more than every existing treatment at `k` 9 in all three. The clause that every existing treatment falls under 50% at `k`
9 holds only for the two treatments a transition page allows (removed and kept) and only where the bias outgrows the noise (five
cohorts pooled, ten times the cohort); the followed treatment, the oracle that needs the year end of every withdrawn rating, stays
above 50% everywhere, and in the first-year cohort all three stay above it. The stability clause is refuted on the real cohorts:
the cohorts' bands contain the pooled anchored `k` in under 80% of the years for B and CCC to C at S&P and for BBB and B at
Fitch. The anchored `k` there (12.6 for S&P's B, 61.2 for Fitch's CCC to C) absorbs the difference between the two pages'
definitions of default as well as any informativeness, so it is a `k` conditional on the two definitions being equivalent, which
ESMA does not guarantee; the capture70 design shows what a definition mismatch does to the anchored interval.

## Notes

- CEREP holds the statistics of the agencies' EU entities as ESMA computes them, not the agencies' own studies; every
  figure names the entity, the scope (corporate, long-term, categories), the period and the definition.
- ESMA sets no common definition of a default event for CEREP, and its three default pages count three different
  things; the case reports each and never mixes them.
- Fitch's EU entity reports its 2001 to 2005 cohorts on its scale before the 2006 change, whose default category is
  "DDD, DD, D" (Fitch's definitions as reproduced in PIMCO Funds' SEC 497(e) supplement of 23 March 2007); its 2000
  cohort is empty and its 2001 transition page holds only zeros, so its history starts in 2002.
- The papers' agency matrices (Israel et al.'s S&P and Moody's matrices, Engelmann's example) are read from the device
  data root and never enter an artifact (derived-only); the case publishes what is recomputed from them.
- Agency PDs by grade are not a bank's PDs, and a matrix estimated here is not a supervisory parameter. Nothing here
  certifies a rating system.
- [Guide 07](../guides/07_pds-by-grade-from-rating-transitions.md) is the same work on your own rating history, with the
  engine's calls, and on CEREP for another scope.
