# Case C05, low-default portfolios and PD calibration

- **Category:** ratings-calibration
- **Sources:** Tasche (2013), *The art of probability-of-default curve calibration*, Journal of Credit Risk 9(4),
  DOI 10.21314/jcr.2013.169 (arXiv 1212.3716v6): the S&P corporate ratings and defaults by grade for 2009 to 2011
  (its Table 2) and its results (Tables 5 to 9). Pluto and Tasche (2005), *Estimating probabilities of default for low
  default portfolios*, arXiv cond-mat/0411699v3: its three-grade example and Tables 1 to 14. Both **derived-only**: the
  PDFs are fetched into the device data root and parsed there; the artifacts carry rates, profiles and results, never
  the S&P grade counts. A Vasicek generator (Contraste's own) for the known-truth variants.
- **One line:** how should a PD curve be carried to a new year, and what can be said about PDs with almost no
  defaults?

## Why this case exists

Two situations every rating system meets. A curve estimated on one year is used in the next, and carrying it there
means choosing what stays fixed: the default profile, the accuracy ratio, the shape of the PD curve or the shape of the
likelihood ratio (Tasche 2013, section 4). And a portfolio with almost no defaults still needs PDs, which the most
prudent principle bounds from the obligors observed without default and the ordering of the grades (Pluto and Tasche
2005). The case recomputes both papers from their own inputs, every printed cell checked, and adds what neither can
show on its own: the capital each choice implies, and how the bounds and the tests behave when the truth is known.

## Formalisation

- **Readers.** `pipeline/io/papers.py` parses the two PDFs and checks them against their own print: Table 2's grades
  must add up to its All row and every printed default rate must be the counts' ratio at two decimals; every
  Pluto-Tasche table must have its six confidence levels and its named rows; Tasche's Tables 5 to 7 must agree with
  Table 2's rates. A reader that cannot satisfy them refuses the bake (CT-201, CT-202). The check found one cut digit:
  Table 7's 2011 header prints 0.752% for 44/5847 = 0.7525% (the forecasts below it use the exact rate).
- **The estimation model.** The 2009 S&P system (17 grades) smoothed by quasi moment matching to its unconditional PD
  234/5860 = 3.993% and accuracy ratio 82.71% (Table 4): alpha 5.2048, beta 2.1613.
- **The approaches** (riskvalidation `engines.pd_curve`): case 1, the forecast profile and PD known (the observed PD,
  as the paper does): invariant default profile, invariant accuracy ratio, scaled PDs, scaled likelihood ratio; case 2,
  only the PD: invariant likelihood ratio; case 3, only the profile: invariant PD curve (the 2009 curve unchanged),
  invariant conditional profiles by least squares and by least chi-square, invariant likelihood ratio.
- **Tests.** For every approach: the default-profile chi-square with a Monte Carlo p-value (`pd.default_profile`,
  100,000 samples, seed 2013), the chi-square over grades, Jeffreys per grade and for the portfolio. For the
  low-default variants, on a hypothetical expert model (0.03%, 0.05%, 0.10% for grades A, B, C): Jeffreys per grade
  and portfolio, the binomial and the Vasicek binomial at correlation 12%.
- **Impact.** The IRB corporate risk weight (Basel III final; LGD 45%, the F-IRB senior unsecured value for financial
  institutions, CRE32.6; maturity 2.5 years; one unit of EAD per obligor), live in the App with the regime and the LGD
  from the rail.
- **The generator.** Grades A, B, C with 100, 400 and 300 obligors (the paper's example), true PDs 0.05%, 0.10% and
  0.20%, correlation 12%, 20,000 independent seeded years; coverage of every bound and size and power of every test
  over them, with binomial standard errors (CT-204, CT-205).
- **Variants.** `sp-2010`, `sp-2011` (published answers); `ldp-published`, the paper's 0, 2, 1 defaults (published
  answers); `ldp-sim-0`, `ldp-sim-1`, `ldp-sim-3`, generated years with 0, 1 and 3 defaults (known truth).

## What the bake found

**Calibration, 2010** (observed PD 1.141%, accuracy ratio 90.15%):

| Approach | Forecast PD | Default-profile p | Average risk weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| invariant default profile | 1.141% | 0.046 (amber) | 60.9% |
| invariant accuracy ratio | 1.141% | 0.079 (green) | 57.3% |
| scaled PDs | 1.141% | 0.042 (amber) | 58.8% |
| scaled likelihood ratio | 1.141% | 0.112 (green) | 55.0% |
| invariant likelihood ratio (case 2) | 1.546% | 0.101 (green) | 61.6% |
| the 2009 curve unchanged (case 3) | 4.318% | 0.042 (amber) | 90.7% |
| least squares (case 3) | 4.799% | 0.034 (amber) | 95.5% |
| least chi-square (case 3) | 5.347% | 0.029 (amber) | 98.4% |
| invariant likelihood ratio (case 3) | 5.377% | 0.029 (amber) | 98.5% |

In 2011 (observed 0.753%) every approach fits the default profile (p from 0.37 to 0.89), and the case 3 forecasts are
2.8% to 3.8%.

Read: with the year's profile and PD known, the scaled likelihood ratio and the invariant accuracy ratio fit 2010 best,
as the paper found; scaled PDs and the invariant default profile are amber. All four case 1 curves underestimate CCC-C
in 2010 (22.3% of those obligors defaulted against 13.0% to 15.6% forecast; Jeffreys red). Without the year's PD the
case 3 approaches forecast 3.8 to 4.7 times the observed one: the invariances fail on these data. Capital follows the
choice: the case 1 risk weights spread by 10.7%, and carrying the 2009 curve unchanged into 2010 would ask 64.8% more
than the scaled likelihood ratio (83.3% in 2011).

Over the 20,000 generated years: from 75% up every one-year bound covers the true PD in every year, several times
above it (grade C at 75%: 2.3 times in the median year); at 50% a year without defaults puts grade B's bound at
0.099%, just under its true 0.10% (coverage 45.4%), and the scaled bound of grade C covers in 46.5% of the years. The
Vasicek binomial keeps its size (4.8% under the truth) and rejects the expert model in 7.8% of the years; the Jeffreys
portfolio test rejects the true PDs in 13.0% of the years at 5% (the correlation warning of BCBS WP14) and the expert
model in 23.7%. The yearly default count's variance is 3.43 against 1.05 if defaults were independent; its moment
estimate of the correlation is 11.9%.

The capital of the low-default portfolio: 23.4% average risk weight under the expert PDs (the 0.05% floor lifts grade
A), 33.7% under the truth, 52.0% to 91.7% under the independent bounds at 75%.

## Notes

The S&P studies themselves are link-only in the source registry; the case quotes the table through the paper and
treats it as derived-only (the counts stay in the data root). The paper's Moody's backtest (section 5) cannot be rerun
from the paper alone. Nothing here certifies a calibration.
