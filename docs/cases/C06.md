# Case `C06`, lifetime PD, IFRS 9 staging and ECL on public small-business loans

- **Category:** `provisions`
- **Source:** real sample: the SBA 7(a) FOIA loan files (U.S. Small Business Administration, as of 2026-06-30,
  U.S. Government Works) and the Federal Reserve 2026 stress test scenarios and history tables; derived documents only.
- **One line:** the IFRS 9 allowance of a public small-business loan book at six reporting dates, each model fitted
  on what was known at its date and tested on what happened after it.

## Why this case exists

It is the provisions case on real outcomes. The question: how do lifetime PD, IFRS 9 staging and the expected credit
loss behave across reporting dates that span the 2008 crisis, its aftermath, a calm year, the 2020 shock and 2023, and
what does a validator find when each date's model is tested on what followed?

The SBA 7(a) loans are small-business loans, not mortgages. The case title names mortgages because the plan's source
is Freddie Mac's single-family loan-level data, and the owner's Freddie Mac account is missing; until it exists the
SBA 7(a) file is the public twin and no Freddie Mac result is published. A default is a charge-off. The SBA file has
no delinquency history, no outstanding balance and no recovery after charge-off, so the days-past-due backstop never
fires and staging rests on the relative PD test alone.

## Expected results

The model ECL coverage should be low before 2008, rise through the crisis and peak in the 2020 shock, then fall back;
the stage 2 share should follow the macro path, since only the PD test moves loans; the lifetime PD gap and the ECL
backtest should be worst where the model of a date did not see the shock that followed. The table below is generated
from the committed documents by `python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c06/page.py`; the test compares it with them.

What the validator finds, read from the table:

- **2006:** the staging rule did not separate risk: the default rate of loans it moved to stage 2 is 0.97 times that
  of the rest (red); almost nothing moved (0.1%) before the crisis.
- **2008:** the realised 12-month losses matched the allowance (1.05, green), with coverage at 1.84%.
- **2010:** more than half of the book is in stage 2 (56.5%) and the allowance is 6.40% of the book, yet the losses of
  the next 12 months were 0.47 of it (red): after the crisis the model's macro inputs kept PDs high while losses fell.
- **2020-06:** the macro shock moved 96.7% of the book to stage 2 and the allowance to 19.64%; the losses that followed
  were 0.07 of it (red), and the lifetime PD gap reaches 19.4 points (red). The model reads the shock in the macro
  path; the losses that a model of this kind expected did not arrive in the next year.
- **2019 and 2023:** no loan is in stage 2 (0.0%), so the SICR effectiveness test cannot be evaluated: without a
  delinquency history, a calm macro path gives the PD test nothing to move.
- Discrimination of the L1 hazard model stays modest across dates (c-index 0.566 to 0.655).

## State of the art: the L3 PC-Hazard rung (S-4)

Tier: state of the art (method tiers plan, S-4, backlog BL-103). L3 is the PC-Hazard model of Kvamme and Borgan (2019,
"Continuous and discrete-time survival prediction with neural networks", arXiv 1910.06724): a feed-forward network (two
hidden layers of 32 ReLU units) maps the loan covariates to a positive hazard rate for each of 60 three-month intervals of
loan age, the last one open, and the likelihood is the discrete-time one of the interval counts. It stands on the same
information as L2 Cox (size, term, sector and revolving flag, loan age as the time scale, prepayment and maturity as
censoring), is trained on the loans known at each reporting date only (a seeded random fifth of them is held out and the
epoch with the lowest hold-out loss is kept), full batch, and its trained weights are hashed in the models document.
What it adds over Cox is the shape: no proportional hazards, a free age profile per stratum.

The hypothesis was written before the run (`S4_HYPOTHESIS` in `pipeline/cases/c06/backtest.py`) and the result is reported
whatever it is. Observed cumulative PD per decile is estimated twice: Kaplan-Meier (prepayment censors; the estimand of a
cause-specific hazard) and Aalen-Johansen (prepayment competes with default; what a book actually shows). Both rungs predict
a cause-specific PD, so against Aalen-Johansen both over-predict where many loans prepay; the comparison between the rungs
is fair because the bias is common. The table is generated from the committed models documents by
`python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c06/page.py`; the test compares it with them.

What the evaluation finds, read from the table (every figure is in the models documents; the run is the six dates, each with
its own training span, 201,438 to 237,899 followed accounts and 5,364 to 31,394 defaults at the horizons observed):

- **The hypothesis holds by the rule written beforehand, at 4 of 6 dates, a thin margin.** It holds in December 2010, December
  2019, June 2020 and June 2023; it does not hold in December 2006 (integrated Brier 0.5% worse than Cox, although the decile
  gap is smaller) nor in December 2008 (Brier 0.8% worse and the decile gap larger, 3.30 against 2.96 points). The rule
  counts dates, not size.
- **The clear gain is discrimination, which the verdict does not use.** The c-index of the 12-month PD is 0.73 to 0.81 for L3
  against 0.56 to 0.64 for L2 at every date (a gain of 0.10 to 0.22). Cox gives each stratum one multiplier of a common
  baseline, so it ranks loans mostly by age; the network lets the age profile of a stratum change shape, and the gain
  measures that the data have that structure (the table does not say which covariate drives it). L1, which adds only additive age bands, stays
  at 0.57 to 0.66.
- **The Brier gain is small.** At the four dates where L3 is better its integrated Brier score is 0.7% to 2.0% lower; the
  score is dominated by the low base rate, so a large change in ranking moves it little.
- **Calibration by decile is better where the book is calm.** Against Aalen-Johansen, the mean absolute decile gap of L3 is
  1.13 to 1.95 points at the four dates where it holds against 1.70 to 2.53 for Cox. The decile table of December 2010 shows
  why: Cox's riskiest decile is predicted at 6.0% and observed at 0.9% (12 months), while L3's is predicted at 6.2% and observed
  at 5.9%.
- **Both rungs over-predict against Aalen-Johansen at long horizons** (36-month gaps of 1.9 to 4.5 points for L3): they are
  cause-specific, so they ignore the loans that prepay and leave the book. That is the stated limit of the ladder
  (`competing_prepayment` false), not a flaw of L3.
- **Limits.** L3 stands on the loan covariates only, as Cox does: it has no macro series, so it cannot condition on a scenario
  and is not the production rung (L1 is). The network is small and tuned by nothing but early stopping on a held-out fifth; the
  grid, the architecture and the optimiser are fixed in `neural.py` and were not searched.

## Validation anchor

The engine's golden values of the provisions API contract reproduce through the pinned engine
(`tests/c06/test_c06_engine_calls.py`); the reconciliation identities of every reporting date close (the allowance and
gross carrying amount roll-forwards, the waterfall, each stage's closing allowance against its model ECL plus the
overlays carried in it), within `1e-6` of the gross carrying amount; a model never sees data after its reporting date
(`tests/c06`); a re-bake with the same seed reproduces the committed documents.

## Notes

- Small-business loans, not mortgages; no Freddie Mac result: the owner's account is missing.
- No delinquency history: the 30 and 90 days-past-due backstops cannot fire, and stage 2 depends on the PD test only.
- No balance and no post-charge-off recovery: the exposure and the loss given default are approximations from the
  approved and charged-off amounts, stated in the documents.
- The adverse scenario is Contraste's own midpoint of the two Fed shapes, not a Fed scenario.
- The SBA files are mirror-allowed; only derived documents are committed.
