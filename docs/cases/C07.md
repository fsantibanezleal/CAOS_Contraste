# Case C07, the Chile provisions trilogy

- **Category:** provisions
- **Source:** synthetic, calibrated to public CMF aggregates (`synthetic-calibrated`): a generated bank, "Banco Andino", that
  is not a real bank and says nothing about any bank. Methods: `riskvalidation` 0.5.0 (tag v0.05.000). Code:
  `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c07/`; documents: `data/derived/C07/`; contracts `c07-models` and `c07-outputs` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** why Chilean loan provisions are not IFRS 9, and how large the gap is on one portfolio, in six scenarios.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The person who answers for a bank's loan provisions to the supervisor and the auditor is asked two questions
that look alike and are not: what the statutory provision is, and what an IFRS 9 expected credit loss would be on the same
loans. In Chile the first is CMF CNCB Chapter B-1: chapter A-2 N 5 of the Compendium does not apply the IFRS 9 impairment
chapter (5.5) to loans and contingent credits, and the CMF's August 2026 proposal keeps one uniform set of provision parameters
while it opens internal methodologies (CAOS_MANAGE dossier 03, D.1 and D.7). The stake is not small: the banks of the system
held USD 7,667 million of provisions on amortised-cost loans at 31 August 2026, about CLP 7.1 trillion (CMF, Informe del
Desempeño del Sistema Bancario y Cooperativas, agosto 2026, Cuadro N° 1), and the ECB found that roughly a quarter of the loan
loss coverage in banks' performing books comes from overlays, with no visible downward trend (ECB Banking Supervision, IFRS 9
novel risks, July 2024).

**Positioning.**

- It computes the statutory Chilean provision, the IFRS 9 expected credit loss and the Spanish Annex 9 coverage on one
  generated portfolio, each with the engine function that defines it.
- It shows the IFRS 9 figure as a probability-weighted measure and each scenario as a labelled component, never as a bare
  number.
- It keeps overlays and the B-1 minimum top-up as separate lines, so a reader sees what the model says and what is added.
- It states that the bank is synthetic and that it audits no bank's allowance.

**Credibility.**

- Why are Chilean loan provisions not IFRS 9? Because CNCB A-2 N 5 excludes loans from IFRS 9 5.5 and B-1 sets the factors
  (the case reads them cell by cell through the engine).
- How large is the gap? On this portfolio IFRS 9 is below B-1 in calm conditions (64.2% at base) and above it in the combined
  severe variant (118.9%), and the case shows which steps make it so.
- Where does the number stop being reliable? The IFRS 9 side uses the case's own PD, LGD and EIR parameters (labelled
  assumptions below), so the size of the gap is conditional on them; the B-1 side is calibrated to the CMF system aggregates.
- Limits: a synthetic bank calibrated to system averages; the IFRS 9 parameters are assumptions of the case; one reporting
  date, so no lifetime PD backtest, stage migration backtest or SICR threshold stability; no compliance claim and no statement
  about any bank.

## Why this case exists

C07 is the contrast the Chilean reader needs and the Spanish one recognises. A model risk function in Chile validates
provisions that are statutory, not modelled: B-1 prescribes matrices of probability of default (PI) and loss given default
(PDI) by days past due, collateral and debtor facts, and individual categories for large debtors. IFRS 9 asks for a
forward-looking, probability-weighted, staged expected loss. The Banco de Espana's Circular 4/2017 Annex 9 sits between: a
supervisory table of coverage per segment that Spanish GAAP banks may use in place of an internal model. Putting the three on
one book, under six scenarios, turns "they differ" into amounts by segment, by stage and by step.

## The synthetic bank and its calibration

Banco Andino is generated from a seed (42): 171,500 accounts and CLP 6.0 trillion of loans in six segments (the B-1
portfolios split by what each regime reads: mortgages by LTV, consumer by product, commercial by assessment). The generator is
`generator.py`; the state of each account (current, 1 to 89 days past due, default) is assigned by quota, each portfolio has
one risk tier whose share is solved so that the B-1 provision index equals the CMF index (the B-1 factor of every account is the
engine's), and exposures are scaled so that mix, 90-day ratio and index match their targets exactly at any portfolio size.
UF-indexed mortgages are converted at CLP 40,873.77 per UF, the value of 31 August 2026 in the SII table
(https://www.sii.cl/valores_y_fechas/uf/uf2026.htm). The parameter file hash, the generator version and the targets are in the
generator record of `models-banco-andino.json` (G.4 of dossier 08: parameters with hash, generator version, calibration
targets, "these rows are not observations").

**Not found on a CMF primary source (recorded, not invented).** The split of the commercial book between group assessment and
individual assessment is not published in the report; the case assumes 40% group and 60% individual (assumption of the case,
UNVERIFIED). Everything else the generator needs that the report does not give is an assumption of the case, listed in the
generator record (`details.assumptions`): total loans CLP 6.0 trillion; arrears exposure 1.2 times the 90-day ratio; annual
default rate half the 90-day stock ratio; interest rates (8% mortgages, 20% consumer instalment, 30% cards, 12% commercial
group, 8% individual commercial); asset correlations in the ranges of Basel CRE31 (0.15, 0.10, 0.04, 0.12, 0.20); recoveries
80% of collateral appraisal plus a case-specific share at 12 and 24 months; the credit conversion factor 0.5 of the undrawn
individual commercial amount; a 20% collateral haircut for B-1 realisable value and Annex 9 adjusted value.

## The three regimes

All regime numbers are `riskvalidation` 0.5.0 functions; the product selects the function for each account and adds up.

- **CMF B-1** (`provisions.local_cl`): group portfolios through `b1_mortgage_pe`, `b1_consumer_pe`, `b1_commercial_pe` and
  `b1_group_provision` (PE = PI x PDI), the group maximum rule `b1_group_max_rule` (no internal method: the standard applies, with
  the reason in the document), individual normal and substandard debtors through `b1_individual_provision` (categories A1 to
  B4, collateral at realisable value and a guarantor of category A1 for 10% of them), defaulted individual debtors through
  `b1_default_provision` (C1 to C6 by expected loss), the 0.50% minimum through `b1_minimum_normal_check` on the individually
  assessed normal portfolio only (A1 to A6, loans plus contingent credits), and the additional provisions through
  `b1_additional_provisions` as their own line outside the comparable total (CLP 3.0 bn, 0.05% of exposure, inside a 0 to 0.25%
  policy range: assumption of the case).
- **IFRS 9** (`provisions.term_structure`, `staging`, `ecl`, `overlays`, `reconciliation`): annual PD by account, converted to
  point-in-time by the one-factor model and to a quarterly hazard; staging by `allocate_stages` at the weighted conditions (the
  origination PD against the current one, the 30 days past due backstop, the threefold backstop, forbearance, watch list,
  default at 90 days); the lifetime and 12-month ECL by `ecl_scenario` at the original EIR (mid-period convention), Stage 3 by
  `stage3_ecl` on discounted recoveries; the ECL of a variant is the probability-weighted ECL over three weighting scenarios
  (factors +1, 0 and -2 around current conditions with weights 25%, 50% and 25%, the worked example of dossier 02 B.4) plus the
  variant's shift; two expert overlays (house price outlook on high-LTV mortgages, card utilisation drift) are separate lines
  sized once at the base and fixed in CLP.
- **BdE Annex 9** (`provisions.local_es`): `annex9_coverage` on the amount not covered by guarantees, with the category from
  the facts the Annex uses (more than 90 days past due is doubtful; 31 to 89 days, forbearance or watch list is normal under
  special watch) and the stage mapping `annex9_category_to_stage`. Point numbers are as of Circular 6/2021; whether they changed
  after Circular 1/2025 is UNVERIFIED (dossier 03 K).

## The variants

Scenario conditioning only: one portfolio per seed; a variant moves the systematic factor of the one-factor model, moves each
account's own fixed draw into arrears or default (harsher variants move a superset), and moves collateral values with the house
price index. Every magnitude below is an assumption of the case; the Federal Reserve 2026 severely adverse scenario is named only
as a reference for the unemployment peak (10.0%, dossier 08 D.1, checked); its house price fall of about 30% is UNVERIFIED there.
No Chilean official scenario path was readable (the BCCh site returned a bot-protection page, dossier 08 D.5).

| Variant | Shocks (assumption of the case) | Factor shift | What moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | none | 0 | the reporting-date state |
| recession | GDP growth -3 points, unemployment +4 points, house prices -5% | -1.0 | PDs, staging, arrears, collateral |
| consumer arrears shock | consumer arrears +3 points | -0.3 | 3% of consumer accounts move to default |
| house price fall | house prices -15% | -0.4 | recoveries, Annex 9 uncovered amount, individual B-1 collateral (the B-1 matrices place a loan by its appraisal at origination, so the fall moves no account across a loan-to-value band; B-1 moves only through the factor shift) |
| UF inflation spike | UF inflation +4 points | -0.3 | 1.0% of mortgage and 0.5% of group commercial accounts move to default |
| combined severe | GDP growth -5, unemployment +6, house prices -25% | -2.0 | all of the above |

The bridge from B-1 to IFRS 9 in the base variant: measurement basis -92.5 (the bank's PD, LGD and recoveries against the B-1
matrices, 12 months, central scenario, undiscounted), Stage 2 at lifetime +17.1, probability weighting of the scenarios +11.6,
discounting at the EIR +9.1, B-1 minimum top-up 0.0, overlays +0.3. The first step is the part the statutory matrices and the
case's parameters do not share; it is the largest in every variant (-92.5 base, -59.6 combined severe) and is shown as one line
because the two methods are not decomposable cell by cell.

Read: in the case's parameterisation the B-1 provision of Banco Andino is the larger one in every variant but the last. The B-1
side is calibrated to the system provision index (2.51%); the IFRS 9 side takes its PDs from the 90-day ratio through a stated
flow factor (half), so the level of the gap in calm conditions depends on those assumptions, while its movement with the scenario
is the finding. The IFRS 9 allowance reacts through staging: in the base variant 7.3% of the gross carrying amount is in Stage 2
and 2.4% in Stage 3; in the recession 40.3% and 4.2%; in the combined severe variant 86.7% and 7.2%, which is why the IFRS 9
allowance more than triples (+235.9%) while B-1 less than doubles (+81.4%). The house price fall (which also shifts the systematic
factor by -0.4) moves the B-1 total by 11.1% and the Annex 9 total by 18.8%; its collateral channel acts on the individual
commercial collateral in B-1 and on every real-estate guarantee in Annex 9, while the B-1 group mortgage factors use the appraisal
at origination and do not respond to it.

The battery on the IFRS 9 regime (rows from `riskvalidation.validation.ifrs9`): the non-linearity of the weighted ECL against
the ECL at the mean scenario path is 7.4% in the base variant (6.3% to 7.8% across variants); overlays are 0.33% of the
allowance (green); the allowance reconciliation closes (largest residual 0); the SICR effectiveness (Stage 2 defaults against
Stage 1 in the generator's own realised 12-month outcomes) is 3.0 times in the base variant, 2.2 in the recession and 2.1 in the
combined severe variant; it is amber in the first two because 86% (base) and 58% (recession) of the accounts that reach Stage 3 come
directly from Stage 1, which the gate of the engine reads as a staging rule that does not see defaults coming (green in the combined
severe variant, where 13% do); the 12-month ECL
against the generator's realised discounted losses is 0.98 (green). The last two compare the model with outcomes drawn from the
same PDs, so they are self-consistency checks, never evidence about a bank. The lifetime PD backtest, the stage migration backtest
and the SICR threshold stability need a history a single reporting date does not have and are not run.

## Notes

- **Roll-forward.** One reporting date has no opening balance, so the 35H and 35I tables are bridges from the base variant to
  each variant (the base variant maps onto itself): stage transfers carry the base amount, a remeasurement line per stage, each
  overlay its own line.
- **Reproduce.** `python data-pipeline/run.py C07 --seed 42` (about 40 seconds on a workstation CPU) rewrites
  `data/derived/C07/` and `data/derived/manifests/C07.json`; `data/derived/C07/SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the digest of each document.
  The tests run a 3,430-account book in seconds (`python -m pytest tests/c07 -q`).
- **UNVERIFIED.** The commercial group and individual split; the Annex 9 collateral haircut table (not transcribed: the case
  states 20%); the Annex 9 point numbers after Circular 1/2025; the Chilean official stress scenario paths.
- **Not a statement about any bank.** Banco Andino is generated; the CMF figures are public system aggregates used as
  calibration targets, with their source.
