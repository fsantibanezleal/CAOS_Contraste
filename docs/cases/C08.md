# Case `C08`, LGD and downturn LGD on public small-business loans

- **Category:** `lgd-ead`
- **Source:** real sample: the SBA 7(a) FOIA loan files (U.S. Small Business Administration, as of 2026-06-30,
  U.S. Government Works); derived documents only.
- **One line:** how the loss given default of charged-off small-business loans moves across six default cohorts, six LGD
  models each trained on what closed before its cohort and tested on it, and what a downturn add-on does to the estimate.

## Why this case exists

It is the LGD case on real outcomes. The question: how does loss given default move across default cohorts, what does a
validator find when each cohort is predicted by models trained only on earlier defaults, and what does a downturn LGD add?
The cohorts span the pre-crisis years, the crisis, the recovery, a long calm and the pandemic.

The SBA 7(a) loans are small-business loans, not mortgages. The case title of the plan names the Freddie Mac loss fields as its
source, and the owner's Freddie Mac account is missing; until it exists the SBA 7(a) file is the public twin and no Freddie Mac
result is published. A default is a charge-off.

**The LGD is a proxy, and its bias is stated.** The public file has no recovery cash flows after the charge-off, so the realised
LGD of a loan is `GrossChargeOffAmount / GrossApproval`: the gross charge-off over the gross approved amount, the only exposure
field the file carries (decision D2). The approved amount overstates the exposure at default of an amortising loan, so the proxy is
**biased low**. A second effect pushes the other way: recoveries and costs are ignored, which overstates the loss net of recoveries,
and capitalised interest or fees charged off can take the ratio above 1. The proxy is never clipped: a value outside [0, 1] is kept
with its range flag (2.1 to 4.0 per cent of each cohort is above 1). A ratio above ten, a unit error of the approved amount (17
charge-offs), is left out by a counted exclusion.

## Formalization

| Parameter | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Variants (test cohorts) | default years 2003-06, 2007-08, 2009-10, 2011-13, 2014-19, 2020-23 | each trained on the defaults charged off before the 31 December preceding it, tested on the cohort and on every later one |
| Training rule | charge-off strictly before the cutoff | the charge-off date closes the workout; the engine's `closed_before` applies it |
| Open workouts excluded | the charge-offs of the 12 months after the cutoff | a stand-in: the file has no default date before the charge-off; none is in the training set |
| Rungs | L1 OLS, L2 fractional logit, L3 beta, L4 hurdle, L5 gradient boosting, L6 Tobit | the engine's fits, and catboost 1.2.10 for L5 |
| Covariates | log approval, log term, guaranteed share, seasoning, revolving, eleven sector groups | all known at the charge-off; the continuous ones standardised with the training moments |
| Beta endpoint epsilons | 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01 | the sensitivity is reported, the ladder keeps 0.001 |
| Downturn | years above the mean plus one standard deviation of the annual charge-off rate | the engine's `identify_downturn`; a cohort is a downturn cohort when half of its years are inside the longest run |
| Floors | Basel III final, four segments | the engine's `apply_lgd_floor`; the collateral is inferred from the term and the revolving status |
| Engine | riskvalidation 0.6.0 (tag v0.06.000) | fits, distribution, downturn, floors and every test row |
| Seed | 42 | the gradient-boosted rung (one thread, fixed settings) is a pure function of the data and the seed |

## Expected results

The cohort mean of the proxy should be highest in the crisis cohorts and lowest in the calm one; models should rank the defaults
(positive rank correlation) while the mean of what they predict lags the crisis cohorts, since they were trained on calmer
losses; the gradient-boosted rung should rank best and no rung should be well calibrated in every cohort. The table below is
generated from the committed documents by `python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c08/page.py`; the test compares it with them.

What the validator finds, read from the table:

- **The proxy moves with the cycle:** its mean is 0.708 in 2003-06, 0.818 in 2007-08, 0.822 in 2009-10, then 0.742, 0.663 and 0.731;
  the mass of charge-offs at exactly 1 (a charge-off equal to the approval) is 12.7 per cent in 2007-08 and 1.8 per cent in
  2014-19.
- **Calibration:** the L2 fractional logit under-predicts the realised mean in five of six cohorts (3.3 to 5.3 points in the
  crisis cohorts, red), and is calibrated only in 2011-13 (gap -0.3 points, green), the cohort right after the crisis, whose model was trained on the crisis losses.
  Part of the gap is structural: 2 to 4 per cent of each cohort has a proxy above 1 and every model predicts inside [0, 1].
- **Discrimination is modest and stable:** the rank correlation of L2 runs from 0.44 to 0.63; gradient boosting has the lowest
  RMSE in every cohort.
- **The beta rung depends on its endpoint adjustment:** for the 2009-10 cohort its mean prediction moves from 0.784 (epsilon
  0.0001) to 0.761 (0.01), 2.3 points, and the document reports all three.
- **The hurdle rung:** it is fitted for three cohorts and reported not evaluated for the other three, because the engine's
  fractional logit does not converge on those samples; no number stands in for it.
- **The floors do not bite:** the mean prediction of every segment is far above its floor (0.10 to 0.25); at most 0.006 per cent of a segment's defaults are raised, an uplift under 0.0001.

### The downturn add-on

The downturn is identified from the annual charge-off rate of the SBA book (no macro path is read). The add-on is the
default-weighted mean proxy of the downturn cohorts minus the long-run mean of the six cohorts; it is an illustration and not a
regulatory estimate.

The add-on covers the 2007-08 cohort and falls 0.1 point short of the 2009-10 cohort, so the estimate is about as high as the
realised crisis loss: a downturn estimate built from the crisis cohorts themselves is only as defensible as the identification of
the crisis.

## Validation anchor

The engine's golden values of the LGD and EAD API contract reproduce through the pinned engine
(`tests/c08/test_c08_engine_calls.py`); a model never sees a default charged off on or after its cutoff (`tests/c08`, the
fitted coefficients and predictions are bit-identical when every later default is perturbed); the distribution counts close and the
values outside [0, 1] are counted, not clipped; a re-bake with the same seed reproduces the committed documents.

## Limits

- The proxy is not a workout LGD: the file has no recovery cash flows, no costs and no discount rate, and the approved amount is
  not the exposure at default. Its bias is downward by the denominator and the missing recoveries push the other way.
- The cohorts are by charge-off year, which lags the default by months to a year or more; the file has no default date earlier
  than the charge-off, so an open workout at a cutoff is approximated by the charge-offs of the following 12 months.
- The collateral of the floors is inferred from the term and the revolving status; the file has no collateral field.
- The downturn indicator is the book's own charge-off rate, not a macro variable; the registry lists the Federal Reserve history
  tables for other cases, and a macro indicator replaces it once C08 is listed for them. The rates of 2004 and 2005 are low and
  2006 jumps, a feature of the file's reporting.
- The SBA loans are guaranteed small-business loans: their loss to the lender is not the LGD of an unguaranteed loan.

## Notes

- Small-business loans, not mortgages; no Freddie Mac result: the owner's account is missing and the slot is declared absent in
  every document.
- The SBA files are mirror-allowed; only derived documents are committed. No loan row, name, address or identifier is in a document.
- The standard errors of the coefficients are null: the engine's fits report none.
