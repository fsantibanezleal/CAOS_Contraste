# Case C09, EAD and CCF for revolving lines

- **Category:** `lgd-ead`
- **Source:** synthetic with a known truth (`synthetic-known-truth`): revolving credit lines generated from a seed, whose field
  structure and marginals follow the Federal Reserve's 2025 hypothetical card portfolios. They are not observations of any
  portfolio and say nothing about any bank or card issuer. Methods: `riskvalidation` 0.6.0 (tag v0.06.000). Code:
  `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c09/`; documents: `data/derived/C09/`; contracts `c09-models` and `c09-outputs` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** whether the credit conversion factor of a revolving line is recovered by the usual reference-date designs when the
  true CCF is known, and how the answer moves as drawing accelerates before default, at six levels.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of credit risk models who owns the exposure at default of a card or revolving-line book has to choose a
reference date, a treatment for values outside [0, 1] and an aggregator for the credit conversion factor, and the validator has to
say whether that choice recovers the CCF the book really shows. The rules do not leave the choice free for long: the EBA consulted
from 2025-07-02 to 2025-10-15 on new guidelines for estimating CCFs under Article 182(5) CRR (whether they are final is UNVERIFIED in
our records), and the Basel III floor on own-estimate EAD is the on-balance amount plus 50% of the off-balance exposure at the
supervisory CCF, 40% for a general commitment and 10% for an unconditionally cancellable one (CRE32.36, CRE32.64, CRE20.98,
CRE20.100). On a USD 10,000 undrawn commitment that floor is USD 2,000 of exposure whatever the model says (source: arithmetic of
the floor on CRE32.36 and CRE20.98; CAOS_MANAGE dossier 01, A.5, and dossier 04, G.2). The data to settle it do not exist: a bank
never observes the true CCF, so a validator cannot score a design against it, and the Federal Reserve's own card portfolios show why
the question is live, with 9%, 15% and 26% of the accounts of the low, typical and high risk files above their credit limit (computed
from the three files below).

**Positioning.**

- It scores every reference-date design, treatment and aggregator against a CCF the generator knows, so a bias is a number and not
  an opinion.
- It names the CCF convention on every page and keeps the values below 0 and above 1 flagged and counted, never clipped, until a
  named treatment is asked for.
- It back-tests EAD in currency by utilisation band, beside the CCF, so a model cannot hide an exposure error inside a ratio.
- It says the lines are generated, and that no statement about any bank follows from it.

**Credibility.**

- Why can a bias be measured here and not on a bank's own data? Because the generator draws balances with a CCF fixed by its
  parameters (a closed form), so the truth is not estimated.
- How far can the numbers be trusted? The fixed-horizon result is exact in expectation by construction; the biases of the other
  designs are a consequence of where they read the CCF, and they are reproduced across 20 independent test replications of 50,000
  facilities with their root mean square error and interval in the documents.
- Where does the number stop being reliable? The acceleration levels, the noise and the default mix are assumptions of the case, the
  Fed files give marginals and no drawdown history, and the limit never changes before default.
- Limits: a generated card-like book with a constant limit, one geometric drawdown shape and a gamma frailty; the Fed files are 200
  hypothetical accounts per tier; no compliance claim and no statement about any bank.

## Why this case exists

C09 is the known-truth case of the LGD and EAD family. C08 judges an LGD estimate against realised losses, which carry their own
noise; the CCF has the opposite problem: it is defined only at a reference date the modeller chooses, it is undefined for a fully
drawn line, it lies outside [0, 1] for fees and paydowns, and the true value is never seen. Here it is seen. The question: do the
fixed-horizon and cohort CCF estimators recover a known CCF, and how biased are they when utilisation accelerates before default?

## The generator

A defaulter's state twelve months before default is drawn from its tier's Fed marginals: an interior utilisation, a fully drawn
line, or a line over its limit. An interior line draws down its undrawn amount geometrically, at a monthly rate `r rho nu` weighted
by the level's monthly weights (1 outside the acceleration window, the level's factor inside it), where `nu` is a mean-one gamma
frailty. The share of the undrawn amount drawn by default is `1 - exp(-r rho nu S)` plus the noise, and the EAD is the balance plus
that share of the undrawn amount, floored at 0 (it binds in under 0.2% of defaulters, tested). A line that is fully drawn or over its
limit cannot draw: its EAD is its balance plus capitalised interest and fees. The six levels of one seed share their facilities, so
they differ only in the drawing profile. Because the frailty is gamma, the mean CCF at any horizon is a closed form, and the truth of
each level is that closed form at 12 months over the defaulters whose CCF is defined. It is tested: at 250,000 facilities the
fixed-horizon mean recovers it within the tolerance at all six levels.

## Expected results

At the fixed 12-month horizon the mean CCF should sit on the known value at every level; the pooled (ratio of sums) CCF should sit
a little below it, because the lines with most undrawn amount draw least; the cohort and variable-horizon designs read the CCF nearer
the default, where less of the undrawn amount remains, and should sit well below it, by less as acceleration concentrates drawing
near the default; the untreated mean at those horizons should be unstable, dominated by the lines with almost nothing left to draw;
censoring and flooring should bias the fixed-horizon mean downward more as the share above 1 grows with the level. About one
defaulter in five has no CCF at the reference date, and one in twelve defaults in the first month of its cohort. The table is
generated from the committed documents by `python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c09/page.py`; the test compares it with them. Each

## Reading the bias

A bias against the 12-month truth is a statement about the CCF number, not always about the EAD predicted at the model's own
reference date. The cohort model, fitted and applied at the cohort start, predicts total EAD to within 1% at level 0 (total gap
-0.2%) although its CCF is 0.258 below the 12-month truth; its error shows in the utilisation bands (worst band gap 25.5% at
level 0, red). A validator who compares CCFs across designs compares different quantities, and one who compares EAD in currency
sees which model is wrong where.

## Validation anchor

The known CCF of each level is the anchor: a closed form of the parameters, recovered by the fixed-horizon design within 0.002 at all
levels (tolerance 0.02), with a root mean square error of about 0.007 per replication. The engine functions are checked against the
golden values of `docs/contracts/engine/lgd-ead-api.md` by `tests/c09/test_c09_engine_calls.py`. If a design that should recover the
truth does not, that is a defect to report, not to hide.

## Notes

- **Synthetic.** Every document carries the notice in its generator block and the truth status `synthetic-known-truth`; the
  Federal Reserve files give the shape of the marginals, not a history of any account.
- **Undefined and excluded.** A line whose undrawn amount at the reference date is not positive has no CCF (22% of defaulters): it
  is excluded from every CCF estimate and counted. A default in the first month of its calendar cohort (8%) has no reference date
  before it and leaves the cohort design.
- **Direct EAD.** The engine clips an EAD share above 1 for the fit and counts it (10,683 development lines at level 0); the model
  therefore cannot predict an over-limit line, and its worst band gap is 57%.
- **Engine solver.** The engine's fractional-logit solver stalled on the pooled development sample of one level (2); the bake
  repeats that fit on every second observation and writes the thinning in the models document. It is reported to the engine owner.
- **Bake.** One bake of the full plan takes about 2.4 minutes; the tests use a small plan (`SCALE = 0.02`) and run in seconds.
