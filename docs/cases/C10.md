# Case C10, portfolio credit capital

- **Category:** `portfolio-capital`
- **Source:** real outcomes (`real-outcomes`) for the loss rates, the Federal Reserve Board's charge-off release, All Banks,
  seasonally adjusted (public domain), and a constructed portfolio labelled `illustrative` for the loss distributions; the
  correlation grids are synthetic with a known truth (`synthetic-known-truth`). Methods: `riskvalidation.portfolio` and
  `riskvalidation.validation.portfolio` (engine contract `engine-portfolio-api` 1.0.0, release v0.07.000). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c10/`; documents: `data/derived/C10/`; contracts `c10-models`,
  `c10-outputs` and `c10-outputs-grid` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** whether the single-factor capital formula, a Vasicek distribution fitted to the Board's yearly loss rates and a
  copula simulation agree on portfolio credit capital, where the 2007-2012 years fell in the fitted tail, and how far the
  correlation estimate itself can be trusted.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of credit risk models who owns the portfolio capital of a loan book has to justify the asset correlation
behind it, and the validator has to say whether the single-factor formula understates the capital the book needs when the cycle
turns. The IRB form takes a fixed correlation per asset class (0.15 for residential mortgages, 0.04 for qualifying revolving
exposures; CRE31.14, CRE31.15) and a 99.9% confidence level; the fit to the Board's yearly net charge-off rates of five loan types
gives a correlation of 0.013 for residential real estate and 0.013 for credit cards on 1985-2006, and a fitted 99% quantile that
five of the six years 2007-2012 exceeded for residential real estate (computed from the documents below). The timing of any rule that
would change these classes is not claimed here (UNVERIFIED in our records).

**Positioning.**

- It fits the Vasicek distribution on one window and places the realised years of the next window in it, so the tail test is
  out of sample.
- It runs every rung of the ladder on the same portfolio and shows where they part: the Gaussian copula, the Student-t copula,
  CreditRisk+, the granularity adjustment and importance sampling, with the Euler contribution of each group.
- It measures the exact size and power of the correlation estimate on a simulator whose correlation is known.
- It says the portfolio is illustrative and the loss rates are those of all banks, and draws no statement about any bank.

**Credibility.**

- Why can the tail be tested here? Because the fit stops in 2006 and the Board's table carries 2007-2012 as realised rates.
- How far can the numbers be trusted? Every capital, quantile, contribution and test is returned by the engine and checked against
  analytic anchors; the Monte Carlo is seeded and reproduced byte for byte.
- Where does the number stop being reliable? Sixteen or twenty-two yearly points identify a correlation with a wide interval, a
  charge-off rate is a net loss rate and not a default frequency, and the illustrative portfolio is an assumption of the case.
- Limits: the large homogeneous portfolio and the IRB form are approximations; no compliance claim and no statement about any bank.

## Why this case exists

C10 is the portfolio case. The PD, LGD and EAD cases judge the inputs of a capital number; this one judges the number a portfolio
model builds from them. The question: do the ASRF formula, a fitted Vasicek model and a copula simulation agree, including in the
2007-2012 tail, and what does the correlation they share rest on?

## Expected results

The Gaussian copula with the fitted correlation should sit above the LHP by the granularity effect, and the granularity adjustment
should approximate that gap; CreditRisk+ with matched variance should sit near the Gaussian copula; the Student-t copula should sit
well above both, because its tail dependence does not vanish with a small correlation; importance sampling should reproduce the
plain Monte Carlo with a far smaller error. The fitted correlations of the consumer and residential series should come out low (a
yearly, all-bank charge-off rate averages the idiosyncratic noise away), and the 2007-2012 years should fall above the quantiles
fitted before them. On the simulator the maximum-likelihood test should hold its size near 5% and gain power with the series
length. The tables are generated from the committed documents by `python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c10/page.py`; the test
compares them with the documents.

## Reading the results

**The correlation and the tail.** The fitted correlations are low except for commercial real estate (0.238 on sixteen years that
include the very low rates of 1997-1998): a yearly, all-bank charge-off rate hides most of the co-movement, and the intervals are wide
(a ratio of upper to lower bound of three to four). Against the IRB class correlation the fitted LHP capital is 0.10 to 0.88 of the IRB
capital. The 2007-2012 years then fall far outside the distribution fitted before them for residential real estate (5 of 6 years
above the 99% quantile), other consumer (3) and credit cards (2), so a correlation fitted on the calm window understates the crisis
tail, and the fit alone cannot be the capital number. The Board's commercial and industrial and commercial real estate series stay
inside their fitted 99% quantiles.

**The ladder.** On the same portfolio, the Gaussian copula's VaR at 99.9% is above the LHP's by 4% (credit cards) to 128%
(residential real estate): the finite portfolio, and for the low-PD residential book the few names that default, matter more than the
correlation. The granularity adjustment tracks the direction (it adds 0.09% to 1.98% of EAD) but, for the portfolios where
CreditRisk+ can be set against it, over-states the recursion's own gap at small n (the formula carries the LGD-variance term the
recursion, with deterministic recoveries, does not, a factor of 1.14, and is a first-order formula). CreditRisk+ with the matched
variance lands within 7% of the Gaussian copula. The Student-t copula with five degrees of freedom is several times the Gaussian
copula (2.6 to 14.8 times): its tail dependence, not the correlation, sets the loss at 99.9% when the correlation is this small, so the
choice of copula is the largest model risk of the ladder on these fits. Importance sampling agrees with the plain Monte Carlo and its
batch-means error is far smaller, but its effective sample is small, so the variance ratio is shown with the effective sample share.

**The granularity truth.** The recursion of the same single-factor model is run against the formula only where the weight on the
systematic factor implied by K lies in (0, 1]: credit cards and other consumer. For commercial and industrial, commercial real estate
and residential real estate the IRB capital implies a weight above 1, the recursion is not run and the document says so.

**The Monte Carlo against the analytic anchor.** The convergence check on 5,000 identical names agrees with the LHP quantile for
commercial and industrial, commercial real estate and credit cards, and does not for residential real estate and other consumer, whose
PDs are low enough that 5,000 names still carry only a handful of defaults; the check reports the gap rather than hide it.

## Validation anchor

The engine functions are checked against the golden values of the engine contract (`tests/c10/test_c10_engine_calls.py`), the
documents against the cross-field checks of the contract (`tests/contracts_u7/c10_checks.py`, run on every committed document), and
the simulator against its known correlation. The size of the maximum-likelihood test at 22 years is 0.052 and at 40 years 0.060 on
400 replications; the method-of-moments test is oversized (0.185 and 0.130) and loses power above the null; the maximum-likelihood
test has power 0.99 at rho = 0.04 and 0.73 at rho = 0.24 with 22 years, 1.00 and 0.97 with 40.

## Limits

The large homogeneous portfolio and the IRB form are approximations (infinite granularity, one factor, a Gaussian copula, a fixed
correlation per class). The charge-off rate is a net loss rate of all banks, not a default frequency of a portfolio. The portfolio is
illustrative: its names, exposures and group split are assumptions of the case, and nothing here describes a bank's book. The
Student-t importance sampling is not specified, so the rung is written as not evaluated. The multi-factor rungs have no closed-form
anchor. The simulator uses 400 replications per cell, so a rejection rate has a Wilson interval of about 0.05 at 0.5 and its
Monte Carlo error is shown beside it. No compliance claim follows.
