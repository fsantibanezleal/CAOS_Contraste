# Case C12, swap exposure, CVA and SA-CCR on a synthetic USD swap book

- **Category:** `counterparty`
- **Source:** synthetic book on real curves (`synthetic-calibrated`): the US Treasury daily par yield curves (1990 to 2025-09-30, `treasury-par-curve`) and the Treasury HQM corporate spot curves (end of month, `treasury-hqm`, a proposed registry row). The book, the CSA, the loss given default, the bank's own spread and every other named parameter are conventions, labelled as such in each document. Methods: `riskvalidation.ccr`, `riskvalidation.regulatory` and `riskvalidation.validation.ccr` (engine contracts `engine-ccr-api` 2.0.0 and `engine-regulatory-api` 2.0.0, pinned by the commit in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`). References: ORE 1.8.17.0 and QuantLib 1.44 (`data-pipeline/requirements-ore.txt`). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c12/`; documents: `data/derived/C12/`; contracts `c12-outputs` and `c12-models`.
- **One line:** how large the counterparty exposure of a swap book is, what CVA it implies, how it compares with the regulatory measures, and whether the simulated exposures would have held against the curves later realised.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of counterparty credit risk who owns the exposure and CVA numbers of the derivatives book has to explain to the supervisor why the modelled exposure differs from the standardised one, and the validator has to say whether the exposure model can be defended against what rates later did. The standardised measure (SA-CCR, CRE52) puts the netting set at an exposure at default of 451,739 USD against 83,936 USD from the internal-model measure (1.4 times the effective expected positive exposure). Both are reported beside the CVA of 9,478 USD. The reason to act now is that the backtest rejects the one-factor model that produces the modelled figure at every horizon (below).

**Outcomes.**

**Positioning.** The case does not say what the bank's own model returned. It computes the exposure, the CVA and the regulatory measures of one synthetic book with a published engine, and tests the model against the Treasury history.

**Credibility.** Every exposure, CVA, EAD, add-on, charge and test statistic is computed by `riskvalidation`; the documents pass the schema and the cross-field checks of the contract before they are written. The engine's Hull-White bond option and swaption agree with QuantLib 1.44 to 1e-7, and the exposure profile of the uncollateralised netting set agrees with ORE 1.8.17.0 within two standard errors at every grid point. The unknowns are named: the book, the CSA, the loss given default and the bank's spread are conventions, the counterparty curve is a market proxy, and the margin-period timelines of Andersen, Pykhtin and Sokol are not modelled.

## Why this case exists

Counterparty credit risk is the part of the validator's job where the regulatory number (SA-CCR), the internal-model number (alpha times EEPE) and the pricing number (CVA) answer different questions on the same netting set. The case puts the three side by side for six variants of one book (a single swap, the netting set, two CSAs, a CSA with initial margin, wrong-way risk), and tests the model that produces two of them against rates that were later realised.

## Data and what is real

The curves are real. Everything about the book is synthetic. The counterparty spread is the HQM corporate spot curve (semiannually compounded, equation 5.7a of the Treasury's XRM methodology) minus the Treasury zero curve of the same month end: an AAA-to-A market spread, a proxy and a floor for lower ratings. ## Formalization

- **Book (decision R1).** A 10 year payer swap of 10M USD (the single swap); with a 5 year receiver of 6M, a 3 year payer of 4M and a 0.5 year receiver of 2M (the netting set). Semiannual fixed and floating legs on one curve; the fixed rate is the par yield of the swap's maturity on the curve date, rounded to 10 bp. The curve date is 2025-09-30, the last backtest date.
- **Model.** Hull-White one factor fitted to the zero curve of the date (`phi`), a = 0.1675 and sigma = 0.00880 from the exact AR(1) regression on the 3 month zero rate between 1990-01-02 and 2025-09-29 (an expanding window; rolling windows of five and ten years were refused because the regression is not mean-reverting at a third of the dates).
- **Measures.** EE and ENE in the t-forward measure (the discounted EE of the engine over P(0, t)), PFE at 95 percent, the effective EE, EPE and EEPE over the first year, the effective maturity (capped at 5 years), CVA and DVA by the discretised formula with the survival exp(-s t / LGD).
- **Collateral.** ORE's credit support amount and delivery amount, a threshold of zero, a minimum transfer amount of 10,000 USD, and a sticky-date close-out: the market of t + delta with the portfolio not aged, delta being 14 calendar days for 10 business days and 28 for 20.
- **Initial margin.** ORE's regression dynamic initial margin, a proxy and never SIMM (the public notice of ISDA SIMM restricts its use to a participant's own transactions), applied path by path.
- **Regulatory measures.** SA-CCR with every intermediate, BA-CVA (reduced), alpha times EEPE, a stressed EEPE on 2007-01-01 to 2009-12-31 (the 5 year HQM spread rises from 0.0057 to 0.0533), and the FRTB GIRR delta charge with the weights divided by the square root of 2 (MAR21.44) and the undivided charge beside it.
- **Backtest.** Monthly dates from 2000-01, six horizons (10 and 20 business days, 1, 3, 6 and 12 months), the PIT by (i + 1) / (N + 2), the Anderson-Darling W^2 with critical values simulated under the model, the effective sample size, three sub-windows, and the 3 month rate backtested as a risk factor.
- **ORE.** Run natively (CPython 3.13 wheel, no WSL), 10,000 antithetic samples, an LGM with the Hull-White a and sigma and no calibration, 30/360 time, monthly scenario tenors; the XML inputs are committed under `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c12/ore_inputs/`. Two ORE conventions were found by measurement and worked around: a coupon fixed on the valuation date is not read in a simulation (the first floating period is written as a fixed leg at its fixing), and a fixing is taken from the next grid date (fixing days and settlement days of one).

### NICA (decision H5), transcribed from the BIS page

Source: https://www.bis.org/basel_framework/chapter/CRE/52.htm?inforce=20230101&published=20200605 (CRE52 SA-CCR, effective 01 Jan 2023, last update 05 Jun 2020), read 2026-10-20. CRE52.17:

> Because both a bank and its counterparty may be required to post ICA, it is necessary to introduce a companion term, net independent collateral amount (NICA), to describe the amount of collateral that a bank may use to offset its exposure on the default of the counterparty. NICA does not include collateral that a bank has posted to a segregated, bankruptcy remote account, which presumably would be returned upon the bankruptcy of the counterparty. That is, NICA represents any collateral (segregated or unsegregated) posted by the counterparty less the unsegregated collateral posted by the bank. With respect to IA, NICA takes into account the differential of IA required for the bank minus IA required for the counterparty.

The definition counts initial margin held, so the NICA of the with-initial-margin variant is the initial margin held at the reporting date (the regression DIM at t = 0, 114,970 USD, a labelled convention: the bank's own segregated posting is excluded); it is zero in the other variants. The fixture's zero was a fixture value.

Declared before the bake and wide on purpose (the manifest `expect` block): the SA-CCR EAD of the netting set between 50 thousand and 5 million USD, its EEPE between 1 thousand and 500 thousand, its CVA between 0 and 50 thousand, the 10 day CSA EEPE between 5 and 100 percent of the uncollateralised one.

## Reading the results

- **Exposure.** The SA-CCR EAD is 5.4 times the model's 1.4 EEPE for the netting set: the standardised measure is conservative for a book this close to par; the stressed EEPE, estimated on 2007 to 2009, is 23,163 USD.
- **Collateral.** The CSA cuts the EEPE to 22,778 USD and the SA-CCR EAD to 158,104 USD (the margined formula floors RC at TH + MTA - NICA); a longer margin period is worth about 1.46 times the exposure.
- **Initial margin.** The dynamic initial margin removes essentially all of the expected exposure (213 USD of EEPE) but not the SA-CCR EAD, which only credits it through NICA.
- **Wrong-way risk.** Doubling b from 1 to 2 raises the CVA from 12,440 to 15,595 USD; the largest exponent b w of the grid is 8.36, so the grid moves the hazard materially.
- **Backtest.** The model is rejected for the book value at every horizon over the whole window, and the exceedances of the 95 percent PFE are far above the expected number. The 3 month rate, the quantity the model is calibrated on, is also rejected by the statistic, but its exceedances are below the expected number at four horizons. A one-factor model calibrated on the short rate underestimates the variability of the long-end rates that drive a 10 year swap; this reading is UNVERIFIED (no second factor was fitted).
- **ORE.** The uncollateralised variants agree with ORE within two standard errors at all 33 grid points. The collateralised variants do not: 17 of 33 and 21 of 33 points are within two standard errors with a minimum transfer amount of 10,000 USD (largest gaps 9.9 and 7.1). With a minimum transfer amount of zero in both engines the gaps shrink to 3.6 and 4.2 combined standard errors and the rows (segment `minimum-transfer-amount-0`) are still red. The cause, that ORE margins on its own call schedule so its MTA acts between the report dates and that the remaining gap is a close-out convention, is UNVERIFIED. The initial-margin variant's expected exposure agrees point by point (32 of 33) but ORE's mean DIM is a different fraction of the Python one; the cause is UNVERIFIED.

- **Findings.** Each document lists its red and amber rows as findings, grouped by what they show and not one per row (S2 for a red level test, S3 for an amber one, status open): the backtest rejections of the book value and of the 3 month rate, the secondary PFE exceedance rows, and, for the collateralised and initial-margin variants, the ORE comparisons. The causes marked UNVERIFIED above stay open findings.

## Limits

- The Hull-White parameters come from one short-rate proxy; the fit to the curve is exact but the model has one factor.
- The Andersen, Pykhtin and Sokol margin-period timelines are not modelled; the close-out is the sticky-date one.
- The minimum transfer amount acts on the report grid in the engine and on ORE's call schedule in ORE (see above).
- BA-CVA: no official numeric example is verified; M is the notional-weighted average maturity of the netting set, UNVERIFIED as the reading of MAR50.15 for a bank without IMM (the BIS MAR50 page could not be read in this session).
- The undiscounted EE is the t-forward-measure EE; the expectation in the simulation's own measure differs by the convexity gap (measured against ORE's own undiscounted profile: up to 5 percent at ten years). The PFE is a quantile in the simulation measure.
- Effective EE starts from zero; the current exposure is the replacement cost of the SA-CCR block.
- The report grid ends at 9.5 years; at ten years every exposure is zero but a minimum transfer amount can leave stale collateral.
- The counterparty curve is a market proxy (HQM minus Treasury), the base-credit spread of the BA-CVA rating is the synthetic 0.0125.
