# Case C13, FRTB desk eligibility: PLA and desk back-testing

- **Category:** `market-ccr`
- **Source:** synthetic with a known truth (`synthetic-known-truth`): desk profit and loss generated from a seed, with a risk model
  whose deficiency is planted. It is not the book of any real desk and says nothing about any bank. Methods: `riskvalidation`
  with its `market` extra (engine contract `engine-market-api` 1.0.0; release v0.07.000). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c13/`; documents: `data/derived/C13/`; contracts `c13-models` 1.0.0 and
  `c13-outputs` 2.1.0.
- **One line:** whether the P&L attribution test and the desk back-test of FRTB catch a risk model with a known, planted
  deficiency, at six settings (none, missing basis, stale volatility, wrong correlation, omitted factor, time misalignment).

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of market risk model validation who owns the internal models approach (IMA) has to show, desk by desk,
that the risk model explains the front office's P&L well enough to keep the desk on the IMA. The test is mechanical: on the last
250 trading days the Spearman correlation of the risk-theoretical and the hypothetical P&L must exceed 0.80 and the
Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of their distributions stay under 0.09 for a green desk; a Spearman under 0.70 or a KS over 0.12 is red
and the desk is ineligible for the IMA; in between it is amber and carries a capital surcharge (MAR32.36 to MAR32.44). Separately a
desk with more than 12 exceptions at 99% or more than 30 at 97.5% in 12 months moves to the standardised approach (MAR32.18 to
MAR32.19). The date is close: the EU application of the FRTB own-funds requirements was postponed to 2027-01-01, described by the
Commission as its final possibility to delay, which is 79 days after 2026-10-14 (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1496; CAOS_MANAGE
dossier 01, D.4 and the EU line of the jurisdiction table; further targeted relief to 2029-12-31 in C(2026) 3647 was not verified as
in force: UNVERIFIED). A bank also needs at least 10% of its market risk capital to come from IMA desks (MAR32.2, dossier 01), so a
desk lost to a red PLA is capital as well as model work. What a validator does not see on a bank's own desk is whether a green or a red is
deserved, because the true model error is never known.

**Outcomes.**

| Outcome | Proof point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A risk model with no deficiency passes the PLA | amber or red in 0.0% of 1,000 replications (95% interval 0.0% to 0.4%), mean Spearman 0.992, mean KS 0.033 | `detection` and `pla` of `data/derived/C13/none.json` |
| A gross deficiency is caught | a missing basis of 65% of the P&L sd is amber or red in 96.0% of replications (red in 57.5%); a P&L dated one day off is red in 100.0% | `detection` of `missing-basis.json` and `time-misalignment.json` |
| The PLA can be blind to a deficiency the desk back-test sees, and the reverse | a model that overstates the factor correlation by 0.3 has a Spearman of 0.986 (never flagged) but a KS of 0.141 (flagged in 100%) and a desk over its 99% limit in 95.3%; a model using the volatility of 20 days ago is flagged by neither test in 1,000 replications | `detection` and `desk_backtest` of `wrong-correlation.json` and `stale-volatility.json` |

**Positioning.**

- It plants one deficiency at a time in a model whose truth is known, so a catch rate is a measured number and not an opinion.
- It reports the size of the test (the no-deficiency run) beside the power of each deficiency, with an interval.
- It says when a deficiency is not caught, and which statistic was meant to break and did not.
- It says the P&L is generated, and that no statement about any bank or desk follows from it.

**Credibility.**

- Why can a catch rate be measured here and not on a bank's own desks? Because the generator fixes the model error by its
  parameters; every PLA statistic, zone and exception count is returned by the engine, so the bake counts them and computes none.
- How far can the numbers be trusted? Each rate comes from 1,000 independent replications with their own seeds; the interval is in
  the document, and a second bake with the same seed reproduces every file byte for byte.
- Where does the number stop being reliable? The strengths of the deficiencies are choices of the case, the desk is one stylised
  two-factor spread, and the risk model knows the true volatility except where a deficiency says otherwise.
- Limits: one stylised desk; a catch rate here is that of this generator, not of any desk; no compliance claim.

## Why this case exists

The PLA is the gate to the IMA, and its two statistics look at different things: Spearman at the ranking of daily P&L, KS at the
shape of its distribution. A model can fail one and pass the other. The question: when the model error is known, which of the six
settings do the two statistics and the desk back-test detect, and how often?

## The generator

A replication simulates 550 days and keeps the last 250. A common volatility scale, known at the start of the day, multiplies two
correlated factor shocks; the hypothetical P&L is the exact revaluation plus a small pricing noise, and the risk-theoretical P&L is
the model's revaluation of the same moves. A deficiency changes only the model: the missing basis adds an instrument basis to the
hypothetical P&L only; the stale volatility scales the model's shocks with the volatility of 20 days earlier; the wrong correlation
maps the market drivers to the factors with a correlation 0.3 too high; the omitted factor adds a third factor to the hypothetical
P&L only; the time misalignment dates the model P&L one day late. The model's VaR at 97.5% and 99% is the normal quantile times the
model's conditional sd. The closed-form Pearson correlation of HPL and RTPL of each variant is stated in the generator and the
tests recover it on 100,000 pairs.

desk exceptions. The table is generated from the committed documents by `python data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c13/page.py`; the test
compares it with them. Spearman flags and KS flags count the replications where that statistic alone is amber or red.

## Reading the results

- **Size.** With no deficiency no replication is amber or red, and the desk never exceeds a limit.
- **Time misalignment** is the cleanest catch: the Spearman statistic falls to zero (red in every replication) while KS does not
  move, since the two P&L series have the same distribution.
- **Missing basis** is caught by both statistics (96.0% amber or red) and the desk is over its 99% limit in more than half of the
  replications, because the hypothetical P&L carries risk the model does not.
- **Omitted factor** at 0.6 of the HPL sd is amber or red in 90.1% but red in only 4.3%: a desk would stay on the IMA with a
  surcharge. The desk back-test sees it in 6.4% at 99%.
- **Wrong correlation** passes the Spearman statistic everywhere (the ranking of days is nearly right) and is caught by KS and by the
  desk back-test, because the scale of the model P&L and its VaR are wrong.
- **Stale volatility** with a 20-day lag is not caught by the PLA at all and by the desk back-test in 0.5% of replications at 99%.
  The statistic meant to break (KS) flags none: at this strength the lagged volatility leaves the ranking and the distribution of the
  P&L almost intact. That is a finding about the tests, not a defect of the bake; a longer lag would be a different variant.

## The PLA complement (BS-13, beyond the state of the art)

The stale-volatility result above is the gap this section works on: a risk model that uses a 20-day-old volatility passes both
statistics of the PLA (0.0% amber or red in 1,000 replications). The model has the right correlation structure and, over a year,
the right distribution; what it gets wrong is when the scale moves. The method is the engine's
`riskvalidation.market.pla_complement` (release 1.1.0, tier beyond: the regulatory PLA and the Sota arms are the other two tiers).
It adds three statistics on the same 250-day pair, never in place of the regulatory zone, and has no regulatory standing:

1. the Wald test of `(a, b) = (0, 1)` in the Mincer-Zarnowitz regression of HPL on RTPL, with the HC3 covariance (it sees a
   mis-scaled or shifted model);
2. the Spearman correlation of `|HPL|` and `|RTPL|` (it sees a model that misreads the size of the day's move);
3. the Spearman correlation of the 20-day rolling means of `|HPL|` and `|RTPL|` (the daily shock is shared, so the rolling mean
   removes it and leaves the two scales; a model fed a volatility `L` days old moves its scale `L` days late).

Their critical values are not a formula: the bake simulates a null of 10,000 desk-years (the no-deficiency variant, ten blocks of
1,000 under case seeds 5013 to 5022, which meet none of the committed seeds), the engine calibrates the thresholds on it
(`pla_complement_calibrate`) at a size of 1% for each statistic and at 1% for the union of the three (Bonferroni, 0.333% each),
and the same thresholds are applied unchanged to the six committed variants (1,000 replications each, case seed 13) and to a
lag ladder of 5, 10, 20 and 40 days on the stale-volatility seeds. The window (20 days), the size (1%) and the ladder were fixed
by the plan before the run; nothing was tuned. The producer is `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c13/complement.py`; it computes no
statistic and no threshold, it calls the engine and counts. The tables are generated from the committed documents.

**Verdict of the plan's hypothesis: holds.** The hypothesis was that, at a size of at most 1%, the complement flags the
stale-volatility model in at least 30% of replications (refuted below 10%). Measured: the rolling-mean statistic flags 99.7% of
the 1,000 replications (997; Wilson 95% interval 99.1% to 99.9%) at a size of 1% for the union, and 99.9% (999) at 1% for the
statistic alone, where the regulatory zone flags 0.0%. The false-alarm rate on the 1,000 exact-model replications of the committed
run, which share no seed with the null, is 0.2% (2 of 1,000) for that statistic at the union size and 1.2% at 1% per statistic
(Wilson 0.7% to 2.1%, a point estimate above its nominal 1%: the union of the three statistics at 1% each reaches 3.3%, as three
statistics at 1% each allow up to 3%; the Bonferroni union holds 0.9%). The refutation bound is far from the measurement. Power
against size (the table above) shows the statistic does not buy power with size: it flags 99.1% of the stale-volatility
replications already at 0.1% per statistic, with 0.1% false alarms.

**The lag ladder.** The rolling statistic flags 57.6% at a lag of 5 days, 97.5% at 10, 99.9% at 20 and 100.0% at 40, against
0.0%, 0.0%, 0.0% and 0.2% for the regulatory zone: the PLA is blind to a lagging volatility at every lag tested, and the
complement sees it from about a week. The Wald statistic is weak against this deficiency (2.4% to 28.7%) and the absolute-value
Spearman statistic is in between (4.5% to 94.1%).

**Where the secondary clause of the research brief is not supported.** The brief also expected the Wald statistic to flag the
wrong-correlation and missing-basis variants at least as often as the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic alone. It does for wrong
correlation (100.0% against 100.0%) and for time misalignment (100.0% against 0.0%), and does not for missing basis (0.9% against
86.7%) or omitted factor (0.6% against 44.8%): the basis noise sits in the dependent variable of the regression and is independent
of the model P&L, so the regression keeps an intercept of 0 and a slope of 1. The absolute-value Spearman statistic is the one
that sees those two (100.0% each).

**What the complement does not do.** The rolling statistic alone is weak against time misalignment (42.3% at 1%; 21.5% at the
union size) and against wrong correlation (4.5%), where the regulatory zone flags 100.0%; the complement is a supplement to the
PLA, not a replacement. The union of the three at the union size, with or without the PLA, flags 99.9% or more of the replications of
every planted variant and 0.9% of the exact model.

**Limits.** The critical values belong to this generator (a two-factor spread desk with a log-AR(1) volatility known at the start
of the day): a desk with another volatility process needs its own null. The engine's page records a check on two other nulls (less
persistent and less dispersed volatility) where the size did not rise, which is evidence and not a guarantee. The size cross-check on
C22 named in the plan could not be run: as the engine's page records, C22's generators produce default counts, scores and migrations,
none a P&L pair; the size is checked on the 1,000 held-out exact-model replications of this case instead. The complement has no
regulatory standing and does not change the zones of MAR32.42.

## Validation anchor

The known truth of each variant is the deficiency and its parameters, and the closed-form correlation of the generator. The engine
functions are checked against the golden values of `docs/contracts/engine/market-api.md` by `tests/c13/test_c13_engine_calls.py`; the
committed documents pass the producer checks of `tests/contracts_u8/producer_checks.py`. If a deficiency that should break a statistic
does not, that is reported, not hidden.

## Notes

- **Synthetic.** Every document carries the notice in its generator block and the truth status `synthetic-known-truth`.
