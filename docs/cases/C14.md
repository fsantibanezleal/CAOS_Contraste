# Case C14, IRRBB: six shocks on the USD balance sheet of the bank that failed in 2023

- **Category:** `balance-sheet`
- **Source:** real call-report aggregates with synthetic behaviour (`synthetic-calibrated`): the FDIC BankFind financials of Silicon
  Valley Bank (CERT 24735) at 2021-12-31, 2022-06-30 and 2022-12-31, each read only up to its date, and the US Treasury daily par
  yield curve of each date. The deposit core share and maturity, the prepayment rate, the term-deposit redemption rate and the way the
  report bands are spread over the buckets are synthetic and stated in every document. Methods: `riskvalidation.irrbb` and
  `riskvalidation.validation.irrbb` (engine contract `engine-irrbb-api` 1.1.0, pinned by the commit in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`).
  Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c14/`; documents: `data/derived/C14/`; contracts `c14-outputs` and `c14-models` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** whether the standardised EVE outlier test and the NII sensitivity would have flagged the position of a bank on its own
  call reports before it failed, and how much of the answer hangs on what is assumed about its deposits.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of balance-sheet risk who owns the interest rate risk in the banking book has to show the supervisor, quarter
after quarter, an EVE loss under six prescribed shocks and an NII sensitivity, and the validator has to say whether the deposit
assumptions behind them can be trusted. The standard test is blunt: the largest EVE loss over the six shocks against 15 percent of
Tier 1 capital (SRP31.82 to SRP31.83). The Federal Reserve's review of Silicon Valley Bank found that it "had breached its long-term
IRR limits on and off since 2017" and that in April 2022 it "made counterintuitive modeling assumptions about the duration of
deposits to address the limit breach rather than managing the actual risk" (Board of Governors, 2023-04-28). The bank failed on
2023-03-10. The reason to act now is that the recalibrated 2024 shock sizes of SRP31.90 are in force in the Basel text from 2026-01-01, so the
book has to be rerun on them, with and without the behavioural assumptions.

**Positioning.** The case does not say what the bank's own model returned. It reproduces the prescribed treatment on the bank's call
reports, with the engine of Contraste, and shows how far each answer moves when the deposit assumption moves.

**Credibility.** The balance-sheet amounts are the bank's own call-report values (FDIC BankFind). Every EVE, NII, shock, test and
Monte Carlo error is computed by `riskvalidation`, and the documents pass the schema and `check_document` before they are written.
The unknowns are named: the split of deposits by customer type, the core share and maturity, the prepayment rate and the spread of
the report bands are synthetic assumptions, listed with their source in every document.

## Why this case exists

A standardised IRRBB result is a function of a balance sheet, a curve, a set of shock sizes and a handful of behavioural assumptions
the supervisor lets the bank choose within caps. The failure of a bank whose supervisor watched the same quantities is the evidence
that the assumptions, more than the arithmetic, decide the answer. The case isolates the assumptions: it shows the measure under the
two calibrations of the shock sizes (2016 and 2024) and under the two treatments of the non-maturity deposits, side by side, for the
three dates before the failure.

## Formalization

**Slotting.** The call report gives balances by band of remaining maturity or next repricing, not by the 19 buckets of SRP31.96
Table 3. Each band is spread uniformly over the buckets it overlaps; the bands the report leaves open above get a stated upper edge
(over 15 years to 30). Bullet items (non-mortgage debt, commercial loans, borrowings, time deposits) sit at their maturity;
mortgage-related items (pass-through and other mortgage-backed securities, residential loans) amortise linearly over their remaining
maturity, and their behavioural flows also prepay at a base rate of 8 percent a year. The non-maturity deposits are split by the share
of accounts of 250 000 USD or less (retail) and slotted by the engine's `nmd_split`: the non-core part overnight, the core part
around the used maturity. Items that are not interest-sensitive (other assets and liabilities) sit overnight.

**Shocks.** The six shapes, the floor, the EVE, the delta EVE, the delta NII of the two parallel shocks, the risk measure and the
outlier test are the engine's. The sizes are the USD row of SRP31.90 Table 2 (2024: 200, 300, 225 bp) and of the 2016 table (200,
300, 150 bp). The floor is zero (the single number of the contract). The term deposits move by the redemption multipliers of SRP31.125;
the prepayment multipliers of SRP31.119 are tabled in the models document and are not applied per shock, because the contract's
scenario cash flows carry the term-deposit overlay only.

**Behaviour.** The requested core share and maturity are stated by Contraste above every SRP31 Table 4 cap (0.95 and 6 years, 0.75 and
5, 0.60 and 5), so that the cut and the `cap_applied` flag show; the behavioural treatment uses the caps. The deposit betas and the
replicating portfolio are fitted on the quarters up to each date.

**Dynamic NII and EaR.** The interest-bearing items carry the year-to-date interest of the report, annualised, as their current rate;
assets reset with a pass-through of one at the month their band allows, the interest-bearing deposits with the fitted beta, the
non-interest-bearing deposits not at all. The balance sheet is constant over 12 months. EaR and EVaR come from 100 000 Hull-White
one-factor paths (mean reversion 0.10 stated, volatility fitted on the daily changes of the 3-month yield over the three years to the
date), the EVE of a path being the base flows under a parallel shift equal to the change of its short rate over the horizon.

## Expected results

The ranges are in the manifest (`expect`) and were written before the bake: the contractual risk measure between 20 and 300 percent of
Tier 1 and the behavioural one between 0 and 200 percent; the model base NII between 0.4 and 2.5 times the annualised reported net
interest income; the deposit beta between 0 and 1.

## Reading the results

The risk measure is the standardised one: the largest positive delta EVE over the six shocks, losses only. The worst shock is the
parallel increase under the contractual treatment and a rotation (the steepener) under the behavioural one, so the answer depends on
which treatment is used, not only on how large the shock is. The delta NII of the standardised method is a gap approximation on net
repricing flows: it treats every non-maturity deposit as repricing in full, which is why it is a loss under the contractual treatment
and shrinks under the behavioural one; the dynamic NII, with deposit betas, is a gain for a rise in rates. The EaR is small beside
the EVaR because the volatility is fitted on the three years before each date, which did not hold the 2022 hiking cycle. A negative EaR
means that the 5th percentile of the simulated NII stands above the base NII of the flat path: the simulated rates follow the forward
curve, which rose at those dates.

## Validation anchor

`irrbb.srp31_92_goldens` reproduces the worked example of SRP31.92 (alpha 0.417, +41.7, +25.4 and -1.6 bp at 3.5 years); the sum of the
EVE of the items equals the EVE of the whole; the engine contract's goldens are tests of this case
(`tests/c14/test_c14_engine_calls.py`). The documents are checked against their schemas and `semantics.py::check_document` when
written and again by `tests/c14/`.

## Limits

The deposit split between retail and wholesale is a mapping of account size, not a customer record. One series (the bank's cost of
deposits) stands for all non-maturity deposits in the fit. The tranche yields of the replicating portfolio are quarter-end par
yields. The realised year of the out-of-sample row is a year of rising rates against a one-step shock of 200 bp, so the row measures
the order of magnitude, not a forecast. No call report exists after 2022-12-31, so the dates of 2022 have no realised year. The
floor is 0 and not the maturity-dependent floor of SRP31.93. Behavioural parameters do not vary by date, so the change-at-breach test
runs on the fitted weighted average maturity.
