# Case C15, IRRBB on a CLP and UF balance sheet: RAN 21-13 Annex 1 on the Banco Central curves

- **Category:** `balance-sheet`
- **Source:** real curves, synthetic bank (`synthetic-calibrated`): the Banco Central de Chile (BDE) table F022, the secondary-market
  yields of Central Bank and Treasury bonds in pesos and in UF, of September 2026, and the UF value of the valuation date; the book is
  Banco Andino, a synthetic bank whose balance-sheet aggregates are calibrated to the CMF system aggregates of August 2026 (the same
  publication as C07). The UF share of each portfolio, the repricing profile of every item, the deposit split, the deposit core share
  and maturity, the prepayment rate, the term-deposit redemption rate and the deposit beta are synthetic and stated in every document.
  Methods: `riskvalidation.irrbb` and `riskvalidation.validation.irrbb` (engine contract `engine-irrbb-api` 1.1.0, pinned by the commit
  in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c15/` and the reader `data-pipeline/pipeline/io/bcch.py`;
  documents: `data/derived/C15/`; contracts `c15-outputs` and `c15-models` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** how much EVE and NII a Chilean bank with a UF position loses under the shocks the CMF prescribes, how much of it is
  indexation and not rate risk, and what the 50 percent offset between the pesos and the UF legs changes.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of balance-sheet risk who owns the interest rate risk of the banking book has to report to the CMF, under
RAN 21-13 Annex 1, a delta EVE against 15 percent of Tier 1 and a delta NII against 5 percent of Tier 1 and 18 percent of the
12-month net interest and indexation margin. A bank above any of the three is a "banco priorizado": it is reviewed in detail and may be
required more capital (art. 66 quinquies). The validator has to say whether the numbers can be trusted. Two features make the Chilean
standard different from the Basel one: the UF-indexed flows form one currency, UR, whose shock is not the pesos one, and the NII measure
adds 2.5 percent of the absolute net UF position, an implied inflation surprise, whatever the rates do. The pesos and UF legs also offset
each other by half when their losses have opposite signs.

**Reason to act now.** The Annex was revised by Circular 2.365 of 2025-07-08 and the revised text applies from the reports due in
December 2025 (dossier 03 F.2, read from the CMF page images). The Banco Central's own report notes that the margins from indexation
fall with inflation under an unchanged UF mismatch (IEF 2025-S2, read in a Senate-hosted copy; the Banco Central URL could not be
fetched by script). In September 2026 the market moved: the 10-year UF yield rose from 2.54 to 2.82 percent and the 10-year pesos
yield from 5.71 to 6.25 percent between the first and the last day of the month (series `F022.BUF.TIS.AN10.UF.Z.D` and
`F022.BCLP.TIS.AN10.NO.Z.D`).

**Positioning.** The case does not say what a bank's own model returned. It applies the prescribed Annex 1 treatment to a synthetic
bank on the Banco Central's curves of one stated month, with the engine of Contraste, and shows what each piece of the standard
(the UF leg, the offset, the floors, the indexation charge) contributes.

**Credibility.** The curves are the Banco Central's own yields, read from its BDE API, and the fitted zero curve is labelled as
Contraste's adaptation, as the Banco Central's terms require. Every EVE, NII, shock, floor, offset, charge, simulation and test row is
computed by `riskvalidation`, and the documents pass the schema and `check_document` before they are written. The unknowns are named:
the bank is synthetic and everything about its behaviour is a stated assumption.

## Why this case exists

A Chilean standardised IRRBB result is a function of a balance sheet split into pesos and UF, two curves, the Annex 1 shock sizes, the
floors, the offset rule and the indexation charge. The pieces interact: a long UF position loses EVE when real rates rise, gains when
inflation surprises upward, and is charged 2.5 percent of its size either way; funded in long pesos bonds it moves against the pesos
leg and the offset halves the sum. The case shows the pieces one variant at a time, on one month of real curves.

**The series.** The F022 table publishes the secondary-market yields of Central Bank and Treasury bonds at 1, 2, 5 and 10 years in pesos
(`F022.BCLP.TIS.AN01/02/05/10.NO.Z.D`) and at 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 years in UF (`F022.BUF.TIS.AN01/02/05/10/20/30.UF.Z.D`). Three have
no value in September 2026 and are left out: the 1-year pesos (last observation 2025-12-24), the 1-year UF (2025-12-26) and the 20-year UF
(2026-08-18). The chosen ones are pesos 2, 5 and 10 years and UF 2, 5, 10 and 30 years. Several are traded on few days: the 30-year UF
has a value on 7 days of the month. The **valuation date** is the last business day of the month on which every chosen series has
a value, 2026-09-03; the whole month is in the raw files and nothing dated after the valuation date enters a fit (tested). The UF value
is `F073.UFF.PRE.Z.D` of that day.

**Why the valuation date is early in the month.** Yields of long tenors are traded on few days. In September 2026 the 30-year UF has a value
on 7 days and the pesos 2 and 10-year yields on fewer days than the 5-year one; the last day on which all seven yields and the UF value
exist is 2026-09-03. The case keeps the 30-year UF rather than the month-end date because without it the UF curve has three tenors, the
engine cannot fit it, and the long end of the book (UF mortgages beyond ten years) would be read from the 10-year yield by flat
extrapolation. The price is a valuation date three days into the month and a calibration month (August) published after it.

**The pesos curve has a fourth point that is not a yield.** The engine's Nelson-Siegel fit needs four points and the Banco Central
publishes three pesos tenors, so the monetary policy rate (`F022.TPM.TIN.D001.NO.Z.D`), the Central Bank's overnight rate, is the fourth
point at 1/360 year. It is a policy rate, not a secondary-market yield, and the document says so in the `source` of the curve (an open
question of the unit report).

**What is real and what is synthetic.** Real: the yields, the UF value, the CMF aggregates the book is calibrated to (loans by
portfolio, loans to banks, debt securities, deposits, equity; Cuadro N 1 of the CMF report of August 2026, the same figures as C07) and the
Annex 1 shock sizes, floors, offset and charge. Synthetic: Banco Andino itself, its scale (its three loan portfolios equal the C07 total),
the UF share of each portfolio, the repricing profile of each item (seeded), the deposit split and every behavioural assumption. The CMF
report of August was published on 2026-09-29, after the valuation date: the calibration reads a month the CMF had not yet published at
that date.

All amounts are millions: CLP million in the pesos leg and in every measure, CLF (UF) million in the UR leg of the balance sheet.

## Formalization

**Curves.** On the valuation date each currency's observed yields are bootstrapped to continuously compounded zero rates at the pillars
(`bootstrap_zero_curve`, read as semiannual bond-equivalent par yields: UNVERIFIED against the Banco Central's methodology), one
Nelson-Siegel curve is fitted to the zero rates (`fit_nelson_siegel`) and evaluated at the 19 bucket midpoints (`nelson_siegel_yield`).
The fit parameters, the RMSE and the series codes are in every document.

**Book.** The items slot into the 19 buckets with the engine's `slot_cash_flows`; the non-maturity deposits go through `nmd_split` (the
non-core part overnight, the core part around the used maturity). The borrowings balance the book so that assets less liabilities equal
the Tier 1 proxy (the equity of the CMF aggregate, scaled), and their UF share is solved so that the net UF position is the variant's
share of Tier 1.

**Annex 1.** The EVE shocks are the six shapes with the CLP row (300, 400, 200 bp) for the pesos leg and the UR row (200, 250, 150 bp) for
the UF leg; the NII shock is 300 bp parallel for both. The floors are -2 percent for UR and 0 percent for CLP. The term deposits move by
the redemption multipliers of SRP31.125 and the prepayment multipliers are tabled in the models document and not applied per shock (the
definition of the outstanding notional of SRP31.121 is UNVERIFIED). The delta EVE of each shock is the CLP and CLF pair with the 50 percent
offset (`cmf_pair`); the delta NII is the largest pair plus 2.5 percent of the absolute net UF position (`annex1_delta_nii`); the
prioritised-bank tests are `prioritised_bank`. The CPI surprise effect is `-PN_UR x surprise / 100`, the formula of the contract (the
engine has no function for it).

**Dynamic NII.** Each leg's items carry a current rate, a spread, a beta and a reset month (synthetic); the balance sheet is constant over
12 months; the short rate is the leg's zero rate at the second bucket midpoint, floored, under the base path and the two 300 bp shocks
(`simulate_nii`). The UR leg is the real rate only.

**EaR and EVaR.** For each leg, 100 000 Hull-White one-factor paths (`hw1f_scenarios`, mean reversion 0.10 per year and volatility 1.00 percent
for the pesos leg and 0.60 percent for the UF leg, both stated synthetic: the days of the month before the valuation date are too few to
estimate a volatility) start from the leg's zero curve. The NII of a path is `simulate_nii` on the leg's dynamic items under the path of its
short rate over 12 months; the EVE of a path is the base flows of the leg under a parallel shift equal to the change of the path's short rate
over the horizon, floored. EaR and EVaR at 95 and 99 percent are `ear_evar` (base less the quantile, positive is a loss) and each carries its
Monte Carlo standard error from `ear_evar_mc_error` (20 batches). The UR leg's amounts are in CLP million at the UF value.

## Expected results

The ranges are in the manifest (`expect`) and were written before the bake: delta EVE between 0 and 150 percent of Tier 1 and delta NII
between 0 and 80 percent, for every variant. A result outside is a finding to read, not a number to tune.

## Reading the results

The measure of the standard is the largest delta EVE over the six shocks, losses only, with the pesos and UF legs paired. In every
variant the worst shock is the parallel rise, which hurts the long UF assets funded by shorter UF liabilities; the pesos leg gains from
it in the long UF variants, so the two legs have opposite signs and the offset rule changes the pair (the offset table shows it, and the
offset variant funds the UF assets in long pesos bonds to make the effect larger). The delta NII of the standard is small beside the EVE
measure: it covers only the repricing flows of the first year, and a large part of it is the indexation charge, which is a function of
the size of the net UF position and not of the rate shock. The floors do not bind on these curves: the closest case is the UR delta NII
under the parallel decrease, and its distance to the floor of -2 percent is in the floors table. The dynamic NII, with deposit betas
below one, tells a different story from the static delta NII for the pesos leg (the static measure shows a gain for a rise in rates, the
dynamic one a small loss) and agrees on the direction for the UR leg, whose funding resets faster than its assets and which loses NII when
rates rise. The CPI surprise is a gain for a net long UF position, and the short UF variant is the book it would hurt.

## Validation anchor

`irrbb.srp31_92_goldens` reproduces the worked example of SRP31.92; `irrbb.cmf_annex1_goldens` reproduces the Annex 1 formulas (the
`cmf_pair` table on both signs, the Hoja 9 table, the indexation charge); the sum of the EVE of the items equals the EVE of the whole
for each leg; `irrbb.nmd_parameter_stability` is `not_evaluated` because a synthetic bank has one estimation window. The engine
contract's goldens are tests of this case (`tests/c15/test_c15_engine_calls.py`). The documents are checked against their schemas and
`semantics.py::check_document` when written and again by `tests/c15/`.

## Limits

Banco Andino is synthetic: its scale, its UF shares, its repricing profiles, its deposit split and its behaviour are assumptions, and
no hedge (a cross-currency swap or a UF forward, which Chilean banks use to close their UF position) is modelled, so the net UF position
of a variant is larger than a hedged bank's. Tier 1 is the equity of the CMF aggregate, above the regulatory Tier 1. The CMF month is
August, published after the valuation date. The valuation date is 2026-09-03 because the 30-year UF yield and one pesos tenor have no
value on later days. The pesos curve has the policy rate as its fourth point. The yields are read as semiannual par yields (UNVERIFIED).
Several tenors are traded on a few days. The dynamic NII leaves the indexation income out, which understates the 12-month margin of the
threshold. The prepayment multipliers are not applied per shock and the floor is the single number of the contract, not a
maturity-dependent one. The EaR and EVaR use a volatility that is a stated assumption, not a fit, so their size is a function of it; the EVE of a path is a
parallel-shift approximation of the path's curve.
