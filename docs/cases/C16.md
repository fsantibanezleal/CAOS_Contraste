# Case C16, liquidity stress, the LCR and the March 2023 run, on the call reports of the bank that failed on 2023-03-10

- **Category:** `balance-sheet`
- **Source:** real call-report aggregates with synthetic behaviour (`synthetic-calibrated`): the FDIC BankFind financials of Silicon
  Valley Bank (CERT 24735) at 2022-12-31, with 2021-12-31 and 2022-06-30 as a time block, each read only up to its date; the Federal
  Reserve H.8 weekly deposit lines of the large and the small domestically chartered banks as the observed aggregate record; the
  Federal Reserve review of the supervision of the bank (2023-04-28) and BCBS d555 Table A.1 as event anchors, constants with their
  source and page; the US Treasury par curve for the revaluation row of the held-to-maturity book. Every deposit-category mapping, run-off
  behaviour and monetisation schedule is a synthetic convention, named in every document. Methods: `riskvalidation.liquidity`,
  `riskvalidation.regulatory.lcr_nsfr` and `riskvalidation.validation.liquidity` (engine contract `engine-liquidity-api` 1.2.0, pinned by
  the commit in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c16/`; documents: `data/derived/C16/`; contracts
  `c16-outputs`, `c16-outputs-reverse` and `c16-models`.
- **One line:** whether the LCR and the liquidity stress models would have flagged the March 2023 deposit run on the bank's own call
  reports of the last quarter before it, and how much of the answer hangs on what is assumed about its deposits and its securities.

## Why this case exists

A liquidity model is validated against what it would have said before an event it did not see. The bank that failed on 2023-03-10 lost
about 85 percent of its deposits in two days (the Board's statement), with an estimated 86 percent of DEP uninsured (94 percent of
DEPDOM). The Basel LCR assumes 3 to 40 percent outflow over 30 days; the ECB LiST 2019 Adverse shock runs 12.1 percent of stable retail
sight deposits over 125 relevant days. The case
puts these assumptions on the last call report before the run and states how far each falls from what happened, with the held-to-maturity
losses that reduced the counterbalancing capacity shown beside them. It certifies no model and claims no compliance: the LCR is a Basel
formula applied to call-report aggregates with stated assumptions, never the reported or regulatory LCR of the bank.

The URLs are in `data/derived/C16/raw-sources.txt`; the raw files stay in the device data root and the bake refuses a file whose bytes
differ from its record. The FDIC file is one API response with every quarterly report of the bank from 2009-12-31 to 2022-12-31; the
reader returns only the reports dated on or before the valuation date. The H.8 pages are the release pages of March 2023 and a 52-week
window before them (a 28-day stride, each page lists four Wednesdays); each week takes the value of the latest release that lists it.
The H.8 record is the observed aggregate of the weeks around the run and never an input of a valuation. The Treasury files are those
fetched by C14. All amounts are thousands of US dollars; the tables below show USD million.

The call-report items are real. Deposits are synthetic in behaviour: the report carries no customer type, so every mapping is an
assumption. The estimated uninsured deposits (DEPUNA) and insured deposits (DEPINS) cover the domestic offices and their matching
denominator is DEPDOM; DEP includes the foreign offices. Both denominators are stated wherever the uninsured share appears. The FDIC
names no call-report schedule or line for any field used, so none is claimed.

## The assumptions, one by one

Each named parameter is a field of the conventions block of every document, with its value, its unit, the label `convention` and its
basis. The ones that decide the answer:

- **Deposit mapping.** Insured deposits run as other retail sight deposits (LCR: retail, less stable, 10 percent); uninsured
  deposits as non-operational sight deposits of non-financial corporates (LCR: 40 percent); foreign-office deposits as non-operational
  other financial customers (LCR: 100 percent; LiST: out in full on the first relevant day). The LCR carries a sensitivity band that moves
  the uninsured balance to operational deposits (25 percent) and to other legal entities (100 percent).
- **The counterbalancing capacity.** The securities book is split by type at the carrying values the FDIC reports; the
  pledged securities (SCPLEDGE) are encumbered pro rata and leave the CBC; each haircut is one minus the Federal Reserve discount window
  margin in force on 2023-03-10 (the table effective 2022-03-14 to 2023-03-12), and a type with no margin takes the 50 percent fallback.
  The monetisation schedule (50, 80 and 100 percent by day 1, 2 and 3) and the duration bucket of each type are conventions.
- **The held-to-maturity loss.** The reported loss (SCHF minus SCHA) is applied to the whole securities book pro rata by type, never
  as a reported by-type loss. The Bank Term Funding Program par valuation, announced two days after the run, is a labelled counterfactual.
- **Run-off.** The LiST variants take the Table 3.3 daily rates of the note on a stated US business-day calendar; the fast run takes
  the calibrated rate on the uninsured segment and the LiST Adverse rate on the others; the calibration rule (the maximum implied daily
  rate of the pre-2023 Table A.1 rows) is a convention, because the dossiers give no method.
- **Borrowings and reverse repos.** Federal funds purchased mature on day 1, the FHLB advances of one year or less are spread uniformly
  over 250 relevant days, reverse repos return on day 1 (synthetic behaviour). No undrawn commitment is in the financials read, so no
  drawdown and no collateral call is modelled.

## The six variants

1. **LCR standard run-off.** The Basel LCR over 30 calendar days on the call-report balances under the named mapping, with the 40 and 15
   percent caps on HQLA and the 75 percent cap on inflows. One aggregate, no daily path.
2. **LiST adverse** and 3. **LiST extreme.** The daily compounding of the ECB LiST 2019 note over 125 relevant days.
4. **2023 fast run.** The rate calibrated on the Table A.1 rows dated before 2023, scored on the 2023 rows; the SVB observation is a
   labelled anchor with the daily rate it implies, not a forecast.
5. **HTM losses in the CBC.** LiST Adverse with the held-to-maturity book at fair value in the counterbalancing capacity.
6. **Reverse stress.** The run-off multiplier at which the survival period falls to 30 days, per base scenario.

## Formalization

The product reads, maps and writes; it computes no liquidity quantity (ADR-0069). The cumulative outflow `1 - (1 - q)^n`, the ladder,
the net liquidity position `NLP(t) = CBC(t) - sum (O(s) - I(s))`, the survival period (the first day `NLP < 0`, or the horizon survived,
never a number for the second), the cliff effect, the CBC haircuts and monetisation, the HQLA caps, the LCR, the reverse-stress solve and
the test rows are `riskvalidation` calls; each document passes the schema and the producer checks of the contracts before it is written.
The reverse stress hands the engine the CBC plus the cumulative inflows less the cumulative other outflows, which leaves the ladder's own
NLP at a multiplier of one.

## Expected results

The ranges are in the manifest (`expect`) and were written before the bake: the central LCR between 0 and 500 percent, the survival
periods inside the horizon, the reverse-stress multiplier of the Adverse base between 1 and 150, the share of the CBC the held-to-maturity
loss removes between 0 and 1.

## Limits

- **Real aggregates, synthetic behaviour.** The case does not represent the bank and certifies no model; the lights are conventions.
- **The LCR is a Basel formula on call-report aggregates.** It is never the reported or regulatory LCR of the bank; a ratio below 100
  percent under stress is not a breach. No public bank-by-bank LCR series is named for the US.
- **One observation.** The March 2023 score is descriptive: one real event, no p-value. The realised outflow is stated with both
  denominators because the Board's 85 percent and the call report's DEP give different shares.
- **The fast run is five days.** A target of 30 days is not below its horizon, so its reverse stress is refused with the reason, never
  a number.
- **The held-to-maturity loss is not reported by type.** It is a whole-book assumption. The revaluation of the book from the maturity
  profile under the Treasury curve is a model-against-reported row, not a result for the bank.
- **UNVERIFIED.** The FDIC licence sentence; the licence classes of BCBS d555 and the ECB note are readings of the notices, not legal
  advice; whether the cliff days count calendar or relevant days; the stress-state transition law of the generator.
- **An empty HQLA stock.** Its Level 2 and Level 2B shares are null with their reason in `shares_reason`, never zero (`c16-outputs` 2.0.0,
  decision X6); the real bank has a Level 1 stock, so every committed document has numeric shares and a null reason.
