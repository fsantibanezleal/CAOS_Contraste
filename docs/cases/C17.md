# Case C17, operational risk: LDA vs SMA

- **Category:** `operational`
- **Source:** synthetic with a known truth (`synthetic-known-truth`): annual operational loss histories of a synthetic bank invented for the
  case, whose frequency and severity are calibrated to the median statistics of the BCBS 2008 Loss Data Collection Exercise (LDCE, BCBS 160,
  derived-only: the four severity percentiles and the frequency the calibration uses, with their source, and no other cell of the tables). The
  losses are generated, not observed, and say nothing about any bank. Methods: `riskvalidation.oprisk` and `riskvalidation.validation.oprisk`
  (engine contract `engine-oprisk-api` 1.0.0, pinned by the commit of the engine branch `task/u12-oprisk` until the release is tagged). Code:
  `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c17/`; documents: `data/derived/C17/`; contracts `c17-models` and `c17-outputs` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** how far the 99.9 percent operational risk capital of the loss distribution approach (LDA) is from the truth when it is
  estimated from 5 or 10 years of internal losses, at four tail indices and two collection thresholds, and where the standardised approach
  (SMA) of OPE25 lies against that truth.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of operational risk who owns the economic capital and the ICAAP of a bank has to defend a 99.9 percent capital
number built from a few years of internal losses, while the regulatory number is the standardised approach: the Basel Committee removed the
advanced measurement approach because "the AMA's inherent complexity, and the lack of comparability arising from a wide range of internal
modelling practices, have exacerbated variability in risk-weighted asset calculations" (BCBS d355, 2016-03-04; BCBS d424, 2017-12). The
loss distribution approach stays as the model of economic capital and of validation. The reason to act now is the shape of the data: in the
2008 exercise 121 banks submitted 10.6 million losses worth EUR 59.6 billion, and the 171,882 losses of EUR 20,000 or more (1.6 percent of
the count) carry EUR 53.7 billion, 90 percent of the amount, with the 20 largest losses alone at 29.5 percent (BCBS 160). A capital quantile
read from five years of such data is a quantile of a few dozen large losses, and the single loss approximation moves from EUR 647 million to
EUR 624 million when the tail index is rounded from 0.96378 to 0.96 (CAOS_MANAGE dossier 05, F.6). A validator cannot say how wrong the
number is, because the true quantile of a bank is never observed.

**Positioning.**

- It scores every rung of the LDA ladder (threshold ignored, truncated lognormal, lognormal-GPD splice, single loss approximation with and
  without the mean correction, scenario combination) and the SMA against a 99.9 percent quantile the generator knows, so a bias is a number.
- It states the collection threshold as a modelling choice: the generator draws every loss from EUR 10,000 and a bank records only what is at
  or above its threshold, so the cost of ignoring the truncation is shown beside the truth.
- It says where the ladder cannot be evaluated: a tail index of 1 or more has no mean, no expected shortfall and no mean-corrected
  approximation, and a fit the engine refuses is counted, never replaced.
- It says the bank and the losses are generated and that the LDCE medians shape them without describing any bank.

**Credibility.**

- Why can a bias be measured here and not on a bank's own data? Because the annual loss distribution is the generator's compound Poisson of
  a GPD, whose 99.9 percent quantile is computed by the engine on a fine grid, agrees between the Fourier and Panjer aggregations within
  0.1 percent and with a Monte Carlo of 10 million years within three standard errors.
- How far can the numbers be trusted? Each cell is 1,000 independent histories with disjoint seeds, and each summary carries the number
  of histories on which the engine returned a capital (`n_defined`).
- Where does the number stop being reliable? Where the engine refuses a fit: the truncated lognormal and the splice are undefined on a large
  share of the heavy-tailed histories (see the notes), so those summaries are conditional on success.
- Limits: a generated bank of EUR 50 billion in assets with one unit of measure, a Poisson frequency, a
  GPD severity above EUR 10,000 and no recoveries; the LDCE medians are medians of per-bank statistics, not the statistics of a bank; the
  SMA is shown beside the quantile for comparison and the case makes no claim of equivalence; no compliance claim.

## Why this case exists

A validator of an operational risk capital model has two numbers that do not answer the same question: the standardised approach, which is a
formula of the business indicator and the ten-year average loss, and the LDA, which is an estimate of a 99.9 percent quantile from internal
losses above a collection threshold. Real data never says what that quantile was. The case draws the losses from a generator whose annual
loss distribution is known, so that each rung of the ladder and the SMA are compared with the truth at four tail indices (0.6, 0.8, 0.96 and
1.1), two lengths of history (5 and 10 years) and two collection thresholds (EUR 10,000 and 20,000).

## Formalization

- **Annual loss.** `Z = X_1 + ... + X_N`, `N` Poisson with mean `lambda`, `X_i` independent GPD above the floor `u_0` = EUR 10,000 with tail
  index `xi` and scale `beta`: `P(X > x) = (1 + xi (x - u_0) / beta)^(-1/xi)`. The truth is the 99.9 percent quantile of `Z` for every loss at
  or above the floor, whatever the collection threshold (the threshold changes what the bank records, never the loss it bears).
- **Collection threshold.** A bank with threshold `H` records the losses at or above `H`. A rung that fits the recorded losses as if they were
  all the losses is biased; the truncated fit uses `f(x) / (1 - F(H))` and rescales the frequency, `lambda_full = lambda_obs / (1 - F(H))`
  (Mignola and Ugoccioni, 2007; Cope et al., 2009).
- **Mean.** The severity has a finite mean only for `xi < 1`. At `xi >= 1` the case states no true mean, no expected shortfall and no
  mean-corrected single loss approximation.
- **Ladder** (each capital is the engine's 99.9 percent quantile): rung 1 the lognormal fitted to the recorded losses with the recorded
  frequency, by Monte Carlo of 200,000 years; rung 2 the lognormal truncated above `H` with the frequency
  rescaled, by the Panjer recursion; rung 3 the lognormal body and GPD tail splice at EUR 100,000, by the Fourier aggregation; rung 4 the
  single loss approximation `F^{-1}(1 - (1 - 0.999) / E[N])` on a truncated GPD fit; rung 5 the same plus `E[N] E[X]`, labelled UNVERIFIED
  (the corrected form is not checked against Bocker and Sprittulla, 2006, or Degen, 2010); rung 6 the splice whose tail is replaced by the
  Bayesian credibility Pareto tail, with a Gamma prior on the Pareto shape from the expert's scenario points (Shevchenko and Wuthrich, 2006;
  the unrestricted prior of decision D4, because the restricted prior would exclude the true tail index 1.1).
- **SMA (OPE25).** `ORC = BIC x ILM`, `BIC` from the business indicator with the marginal coefficients 12, 15 and 18 percent, `ILM = ln(e - 1 +
  (LC / BIC)^0.8)`, `LC` fifteen times the average annual loss over the last ten years (the years available when there are five to nine);
  with fewer than five years only the BIC applies and the case never computes an ILM on so little data. The synthetic bank has a stated
  business indicator of EUR 1.4 billion (bucket 2, BIC EUR 180 million).

## The generator

| Tail index | Scale above the floor (EUR) | Losses a year from EUR 10,000 | Largest log error against the four LDCE medians |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6 | 24,382 | 59.2 | 0.447 |
| 0.8 | 17,744 | 65.3 | 0.215 |
| 0.96 | 12,824 | 73.4 | 0.038 |
| 1.1 | 8,797 | 85.7 | 0.160 |

The scale is the only free parameter: the least squares fit, on the log excess quantiles over EUR 20,000, of a GPD with the tail index of the
variant to the LDCE medians 27,632, 41,916, 82,608 and 418,400. A tail index of 0.96 reproduces the four medians (the dossier's own fit gives
0.96378); a tail index of 0.6 cannot reach the 95th percentile (largest log error 0.45), which is stated, not hidden. The frequency
is Poisson, set so that the expected count of losses of EUR 20,000 or more is 0.82 per EUR billion of assets (the LDCE median) times the
EUR 50 billion of the bank, 41 a year. Each replication is a pure function of its seed (Poisson counts, GPD amounts by the engine's inverse
cdf, then the labels date, event type and business line); its rows pass the `loss_events` family of contract 1 before any estimate. The seeds
of the 16 cells never overlap.

## Reading the results

- **Ratio to the truth.** Each cell is the median over the histories of the 99.9 percent capital divided by the true quantile of the variant,
  with the quartiles. A median below 1 is an understatement; the quartiles are the dispersion a validator would see across banks of that
  history.
- **The collection threshold.** Rung 1 ignores it and rungs 2 to 6 model it. The threshold of EUR 20,000 leaves fewer losses than EUR 10,000,
  so it widens the dispersion of every rung that fits the recorded losses.
- **The SMA column.** The SMA capital over the true quantile: it depends on the losses only through the loss component, so it does not
  follow the tail index; the case compares it with the quantile and claims no equivalence.
- **n_defined.** The number of histories on which the engine returned the capital of a rung is in each summary; a rung the engine refuses on
  a history (a fit that did not converge, a splice whose body weight is 1) is not replaced by a number.

## Validation anchor

The engine is checked against Shevchenko's benchmark (Poisson(100) with LN(0, 2): mean 738.9056, variance 298095.7987, skewness 40.3428, and
the 99.9 percent quantile 5849 by the Panjer recursion and the Fourier aggregation, 5853 by Monte Carlo) in every document as the test
`oprisk.benchmark_reproduction`, and against the goldens of `docs/contracts/engine/oprisk-api.md` by `tests/c17/test_c17_engine_calls.py`.
If a rung that should recover the truth does not, that is a finding to report, not to hide.

## Notes

- **Synthetic.** Every document carries the notice in its truth, bank and generator blocks and the truth status `synthetic-known-truth`; the
  LDCE medians are medians of per-bank statistics and describe no bank.
- **Undefined rungs.** The engine's maximum likelihood fit of a truncated lognormal to heavy-tailed data can run to a degenerate maximum (a
  mean of minus several hundred thousand and a scale of several hundred, flagged converged), and the splice fit can return a body weight of
  1; the engine then refuses the rescaling or the aggregation and the history counts as undefined for that rung. At tail index 1.1 the
  truncated lognormal is defined on 205 of 1,000 histories in the 10-year cell with the EUR 20,000
  threshold and the splice on 409; at tail index 0.6 the splice is defined on 859. These
  summaries are conditional on the fit succeeding. Both are reported to the engine owner.
- **Scenario points.** The expert states the true severity quantile of eight exceedance frequencies from four a year to one in two and a half
  years, rounded to two significant figures, and the true frequency above EUR 100,000; the prior is the expert's and reads no loss of any
  history. The bootstrap seed is the first whose refits all lie inside the engine's search bounds. The largest influence of one point on the
  capital of the first 10-year history is +8.3% (S1).
- **Bake.** The replications of the 16 cells (16,000 histories) take about 70 minutes on ten processes of the device, the true quantiles
  and the single-history blocks a few minutes more; the tests use a small plan and run in minutes; the bake is local, never in CI (ADR-0074).
