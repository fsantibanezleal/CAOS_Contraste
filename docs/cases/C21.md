# Case C21, fair-lending disparity testing on public HMDA decisions, Delaware, 2018 to 2024

- **Category:** `ml-ai-risk`
- **Source:** real public decisions (`real-outcomes`: the decisions are real, there is no loan outcome): the HMDA loan-level rows of
  Delaware for the activity years 2018 to 2024, from the CFPB / FFIEC Data Browser CSV endpoint (source `hmda`, mirror-allowed, the data
  licence sentence UNVERIFIED). Methods: `riskvalidation.fairness` and `riskvalidation.validation.fairness` (engine contract
  `engine-fairness-api` 1.2.0, pinned by the commit in `data-pipeline/requirements.txt`). Code: `data-pipeline/pipeline/cases/c21/`;
  documents: `data/derived/C21/`; contracts `c21-outputs` and `c21-models` 1.0.0.
- **One line:** whether approval disparities by protected class exist in public HMDA decisions, whether they persist after the permitted
  controls, and whether the finding is stable from one year to the next. Statistical disparity only: no legal claim.

## Executive brief

**Initiative.** The head of fair-lending compliance, with the model validator, owns the question a regulator and a plaintiff both ask of a
mortgage book: do approval rates differ by race, ethnicity, sex or age, and does the gap survive the underwriting variables? The public
record that anyone can read is HMDA. The legal ground moved: Regulation B as amended effective 2026-07-21 states "The Act does not provide
that the 'effects test' applies for determining whether there is discrimination in violation of the Act" (12 CFR 1002.6(a), 91 FR 21668),
while the Fair Housing Act is a separate statute under which the Supreme Court held that "disparate-impact claims are cognizable" (91 FR 21620). The reason to act now is that the
test a lender runs on its own book has to be separated from the legal theory it serves, and the public data show what an outside reader
sees first.

**Outcomes.**

| The finding is stable from one year to the next | the controlled estimate refitted on t + 1 has the same sign in 71 of 78 contrasts, and 6 of 6 for Black applicants, with size ratios from 0.83 to 1.11 | `contrasts[].confirmation` |

**Positioning.** The case does not say that any lender discriminated. It reproduces on public data the tests a validator would run, with
the engine of Contraste, and shows how far each answer moves when the controls and the year move. Every number carries "no legal claim".

**Credibility.** The rows are the CFPB / FFIEC public data, and the counts for 2024 (24,306 originations and 8,600 denials) agree with
the Data Browser's own aggregation. Every rate, test, interval and replication is computed by `riskvalidation`, and the documents pass
the schema and `check_c21` before they are written. The unknowns are named: the credit score is absent from the public data, and
the data licence sentence is UNVERIFIED.

## Why this case exists

A fair-lending review asks three things of one set of decisions: is there a gap, does it survive the controls the lender is allowed to
use, and does it repeat. The first is arithmetic on counts. The second needs a model that holds the underwriting variables fixed and
says what it cannot hold fixed. The third needs a second year. Public HMDA is the only dataset in the product that has real decisions
about people, so the case is also where the product is most careful: it measures disparity, it scores nobody, and it says "no legal
claim" on every view.

What is real: the decisions, the groups, the amounts and the terms as the public release states them. What is modified by the release:
the loan identifier, the application and action dates and the credit score are withheld; the loan amount and the property value are
rounded to the midpoint of a $10,000 interval; the debt-to-income ratio is exact from 36 to 49 percent and binned outside; the age is
binned. What is a convention, labelled so in every document:

- the decision: approval is action taken 1 and 2, denial is 3; codes 4 to 8 are left out and counted by code (no source classifies them);
- the identity: `id` is `<activity_year>-<row>` and the observation date is `<activity_year>-12-31`;
- the Joint, Not Available and Free Form Text Only categories are their own groups, left out of every pairwise test;
- the debt-to-income field is recoded to six published bins; the reference group of each attribute (White, Not Hispanic or Latino,
  Male, and 35-44 for age) is the customary comparison group, a convention, labelled so in every document (no source names a reference age
  band);
- the design of the controls (see Formalization).

The data licence sentence is UNVERIFIED. The documents quote the platform software terms, which cover the software package and not the
published datasets, and the CFPB statement that "the publicly available data are modified to protect applicants' and borrowers' privacy".

## Formalization

Per year pair, fitted on year t and confirmed on t + 1, and per attribute (race, ethnicity, sex, age):

1. the approval rate of each group, the parity gap and ratio against the reference group, the adverse impact ratio, and the four-fifths
   flag of each group against the highest-rate group, decided on the counts in exact arithmetic (a group is flagged when
   `5 x_g n_h < 4 x_h n_g`; 60 of 100 against 75 of 100 is exactly four-fifths and is not flagged);
2. the pooled two-proportion z-test and the Fisher exact test of each group against the reference group;
3. the controlled decision estimate: a logistic model of the approval on the protected indicator and the permitted controls, fitted on
   the rows of the two groups, with the Wald interval of the indicator; and the rate-spread estimate: a linear model of the rate spread
   in percentage points on the same terms, for the loans of codes 1 and 2;
4. the confirmation: the same estimates refitted on year t + 1, compared with the fit by sign and by the ratio of the sizes;
5. situation testing of Black or African American applicants against White applicants (decision G15: the attribute with the persistent
   gap), ceteris paribus comparator only, k = 30, alpha 0.05, tau 0, the range normalization,
   centers excluded; the counterfactual test is not run because public HMDA gives no counterfactual dataset.
6. the findings of each pair document, statistical findings about its own test rows, each titled "no legal claim", naming its direction in
   words and none stating a violation: S2 `open`, a repeating controlled disparity (95 percent interval excluding 0, sign repeated on
   t + 1) where the group is approved at lower controlled odds than its reference group; S3 `open`, the repeating ones at higher
   controlled odds, a controlled disparity that does not repeat, and a repeating rate-spread disparity with a higher spread for the group;
   S4 `open`, the unconditional gap as an indicator only, with the sentence of the Interagency Procedures, which also takes the repeating
   rate-spread disparities with a lower spread; S4 `accepted`, the stated limits (the credit score is missing, the complete-case
   selection). The severities run from S1 critical to S4 minor.

The permitted controls are the debt-to-income bin (indicators), the natural logarithm of the combined loan-to-value ratio, the income, the
loan amount and the property value, and the indicators of the loan type, the loan purpose, the lien status and the occupancy type. A row with
a control missing or not positive is left out of the controlled fits and counted; an indicator that is constant in the pair of groups, or
whose rows all share one decision, is dropped. No outcome-conditioned test (equalized odds, equal opportunity, calibration within
groups, AUC by group) runs: HMDA has no outcome.

## Expected results

Declared before the bake and wide on purpose: the approval share of the decision rows of any year lies between 0.55 and 0.95, and the
parity ratio of Black or African American applicants against White applicants lies between 0.50 and 1.20. A result outside is a finding to
read, not a range to tune.

## Reading the results

The unconditional rates are indicators only: the Interagency Fair Lending Examination Procedures state that "gross HMDA denial or approval
rate disparities are not appropriate for disproportionate adverse impact analysis because they typically cannot be attributed to a specific
policy or criterion". The controlled estimate is an approximation of comparative evidence: it holds fixed the nine public underwriting
fields and nothing else, it omits the credit score and every non-public factor, and it is never a finding of a violation. A group whose
interval excludes 0 is a group to look at with the lender's own data, not a lender to accuse.

The replication is a test of sign and size. A confirmation with the same sign and a size ratio near 1 says the estimate did not move
between two years; it does not say the estimate is right. Small groups (American Indian or Alaska Native, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific
Islander, 2 or more minority races) have wide intervals and size ratios that swing; read their intervals before their points.

## Validation anchor

Every number of a document equals a `riskvalidation` call on the inputs the document carries. The independent references are scipy and
statsmodels: the z-test against `proportions_ztest`, the Fisher exact test against `scipy.stats.fisher_exact`, the logistic fit against
statsmodels `Logit` (agreement to 1e-6 on the coefficient) and the rate-spread fit against `OLS` (1e-8), each on planted synthetic
files with a known race effect that the estimators recover. The documents pass `tests/contracts_u14/semantics.py::check_c21`.

## Limits

- One state. Delaware is the state of the dossier's probe; no national claim is made, and a pair is a statement about Delaware.
- No credit score, no appraisal, no lender identity: a gap that survives the controls may still be explained by what the public data
  omit. The controlled estimates pool every lender.
- The one-year and three-year Data Browser products differ in the revisions they incorporate; each file is pinned by its SHA-256.
- The controls are linear in the logarithms of the continuous fields and in the bins of the debt-to-income ratio; the exact values 36 to 49
  are one bin. Another functional form would move the estimates.
- Rows with a control missing (the debt-to-income ratio, the loan-to-value ratio, the property value or the income) are left out of the
  controlled fits, and denied applications miss them more often than originated loans; the unconditional rates use every decision row.
- Situation testing runs on one attribute (race, Black or African American against White) because the contract holds one situation
  block. Its distance uses the range normalization
  of the paper's text; the reference code's standardization would give other counts.
- The unconditional rates, the tests and the flags are computed on the fit year of each pair, so the confirm year of the last pair
  (2024) appears only through its confirmation of the controlled estimates.
- The case states no legal conclusion. Ethnicity maps to national origin only by convention (UNVERIFIED).
