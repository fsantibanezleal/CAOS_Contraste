# Case C22, validating the validator: the size and power of every test

- **Category:** validator
- **Sources:** no data. Every variant is drawn from `riskvalidation` 0.3.2's generators (`DefaultCounts`,
  `BinormalScores`, `LogisticPortfolio`, `TwoSample`, `Migrations`, `GradeFrequencies`, `NestedLda`) with its
  configuration, truth and seed committed in the artifact (truth status `synthetic-known-truth`). Three published
  simulation studies are the answer key for the harness: BCBS Working Paper 14 (2005), Tables 5 to 8, and Yurdakul and
  Naranjo (2020), *Statistical properties of the population stability index*, Journal of Risk Model Validation 14(4),
  DOI 10.21314/JRMV.2020.227, Table 4, both **derived-only** (the PDFs are fetched into the device data root and
  parsed there; the artifacts carry the published rates beside the measured ones, never the PDFs); Demler, Pencina
  and D'Agostino (2012), DOI 10.1002/sim.5328, quoted for one published rate.
- **One line:** how often does each test reject a model that is right, and how often does it catch a model that is
  wrong in a given way?

## Why this case exists

A validator reads p-values every day, and each one is evidence only as far as the test's size (how often it rejects a
right model) and power (how often it catches a given defect) are known. With real outcomes neither is measurable,
because the truth is unknown. On generated data whose truth is known both are, and the measurement is itself an
experiment with an error that must be reported (Morris, White and Crowther 2019, DOI 10.1002/sim.8086, who found that
93 of 100 simulation studies in one volume of Statistics in Medicine reported no Monte Carlo standard error). The case
measures every test with a p-value that Contraste has built so far, reruns the published simulations those tests come
with, and changes the product where a measurement said a test was being used wrongly.

## Formalisation

- **The harness** (`riskvalidation.harness`, documented on the engine's Size and power page). A rejection rate
  $\hat r = \frac1{n}\sum_i \mathbb 1(p_i < \alpha)$ over $n$ seeded repetitions, each drawn from its own random stream
  (`SeedSequence(seed).spawn(n)`), with its Monte Carlo standard error $\sqrt{\hat r(1-\hat r)/n}$ (Morris et al.,
  Table 6) and the Wilson interval (Brown, Cai and DasGupta 2001). A test holds its size when its rate under the null
  at its boundary is at most $\alpha + 3.09\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)/n}$, 6.06% at 5% and 1.39% at 1% with 4,000
  repetitions; where its rejection probability is exact, when that probability is at most the level.
- **Exact rejection probabilities.** The binomial, Vasicek-corrected binomial and Jeffreys tests see a portfolio only
  through its default count, so each rejects exactly from a critical count $k^*$ (found by bisection on the test
  itself), and under a true PD $\pi$ and asset correlation $\rho$
  $P(\text{reject}) = \int \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n, p(x)) \ge k^*]\,\varphi(x)\,dx$ with
  $p(x) = \Phi\big((\Phi^{-1}(\pi) - \sqrt\rho\,x)/\sqrt{1-\rho}\big)$, integrated on a 256-node Gauss-Hermite rule. The
  traffic lights over independent years have their exact probability by convolving each year's colour probabilities.
  Every simulated rate of these tests is held to its exact value within 3.29 standard errors (CT-303).
- **The readers.** `pipeline/io/papers.py` parses WP14's Tables 5 to 8 (the eight scenarios with their 16 parameters
  each, Table 6's forecasts equal to Table 5's, every rate in [0, 1], 25,000 runs stated in footnote 24) and Yurdakul
  and Naranjo's Table 4 (its six pairs of sample sizes, the benchmark near 5% without a shift, 10,000 runs stated), and
  refuses the bake otherwise.
- **The families** (severity 0 is the null, 1 to 5 the planted defect; the ladders are Contraste's design choices, set
  from pilot runs so that the targeted tests' power runs from their size to near one):

| Family | Generator | Ladder | Tests | Repetitions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `null` | one per test, its null at the boundary | none | all 17 tests with a p-value; five settings that matter (below) | 4,000 |
| `miscalibration` | `DefaultCounts`, `LogisticPortfolio` | true PD over the PD applied: 1, 1.1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3 | binomial, Jeffreys, Vasicek-corrected, chi-square over grades, default profile, Hosmer-Lemeshow, Spiegelhalter, normal and traffic lights over five years | 2,000 |

| `drift` | `TwoSample`, `LogisticPortfolio(shift)` | mean shift: 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5 SD | PSI benchmark, chi-square, Kolmogorov-Smirnov; Hosmer-Lemeshow and Spiegelhalter with a right model | 2,000; the paper's 10,000 for its table |

Fifteen of the seventeen hold their size at both levels. The normal test over five years rejects above its level, as
WP14's own Table 7 shows (finding F-NORMAL-SIZE); Jeffreys, a Bayesian posterior probability read as a p-value, has
an exact size of 1.38% at 1% for these 1,000 obligors (F-JEFFREYS-DISCRETE). The migration z-tests and the HHI test
are far below their level: the first because Holm's correction spans 42 cells, the second because its statistic is
bounded (below). The ECB AUC test is a little conservative when the development AUC is the true one.

**Power against the planted defects** (rate at 5%, 2,000 repetitions; exact in brackets):

- *Miscalibration* (5,000 obligors at a PD applied of 2% for the count tests): the binomial test catches a PD 25% too
  low 76.0% of the time (exact 74.9%), Jeffreys 79.7% (77.8%), and the Jeffreys test reaches 80% power at a
  true-to-applied ratio of 1.2587 (exact); the Vasicek-corrected test, assuming a 12% correlation the defaults do not
  have, catches nothing until the PD is three times too low (79.0%; finding F-VASICEK-COST). The default-profile test
  stays at its size (2.4% at a ratio of 3): it sees the shape of the PD curve, not its level (F-PROFILE-BLIND).
  Hosmer-Lemeshow reaches 64.9% when every odds is 1.25 times too low, Spiegelhalter 94.6%.
- *Default clustering* (the PDs right; 1,000 obligors at PD 1% for the count tests): the binomial test's true size at
  5% rises to 17.5% with a 5% asset correlation and about 19% from 10% on (exact 19.8% at 12%); the chi-square over grades rejects right
  PDs 74.9% of the time at 10%, Hosmer-Lemeshow 77.7% (F-BINOM-RHO, F-OBLIGOR-RHO). The Vasicek-corrected test holds
  its size up to the correlation it assumes. WP14's normal test, which estimates the variance across years, does not
  inflate: 8.2% without correlation, 3.5% at 20% with a factor correlation of 0.2 across years.
- *Drift* (samples of 1,000): the PSI benchmark catches a 0.1 SD shift 27.2% of the time and a 0.2 SD shift 89.6%;
  Kolmogorov-Smirnov is the most powerful (46.5% and 97.3%); PSI > 0.25 never fires on a 0.2 SD shift
  (F-PSI-BANDS). A right model under a 0.5 SD covariate shift keeps Hosmer-Lemeshow at 4.85%: drift is not
  miscalibration (F-DRIFT-NOT-MISCAL).
- *Discrimination decay* (150 defaulters): the ECB test reaches 80% power at an AUC drop of 0.049, DeLong against a
  challenger at 0.060; a two-point drop is caught 25.5% of the time (F-DECAY-POWER). With the development AUC
  estimated on 0.467 times the current sample (C01's slice ratio) the published form rejects 17.0% of the time when
  nothing changed; the extension 5.3% (F-AUC-INIT-C01).
- *Leakage*: a leak of 1 inflates the development AUC by 0.150 (exact), and the ECB test out of time catches it every
  time, while the CSI of the leaked feature sees it 14.6% of the time (F-LEAK-SIGNAL). DeLong on Demler's nested design
  rejects 0.2% of the time; on two non-nested scores of equal AUC 5.2% (F-DELONG-NESTED).
- *Broken monotonicity*: the migration z-tests miss a cell twice its closer neighbour 44.5% of the time with 500
  obligors per grade, and catch it always with 2,000 (F-MIGRATION-POWER); with grades 3 and 4's PDs swapped the
  default profile rejects 95.2% of the time, the chi-square over grades 93.7%.
- *Concentration*: the ECB HHI test never rejects, even with half of every grade's mass moved into the modal grade;
  its statistic peaks at 1.0913 for these ten grades, below the 1.645 a 5% rejection needs. The chi-square of
  homogeneity on the counts catches a 5% shift 94.8% of the time (F-HHI-BOUNDED).

## Validation anchor

The exact rejection probabilities (every simulated rate of the count tests and the traffic lights agrees with its
exact value within 3.29 SE), the three published studies cell by cell, and the live calculator held to the engine on
exported parity points within 1e-9 (CT-308).

## Notes

A test that holds its size on a generator holds it for that generator's assumptions, not for every portfolio; each
rate names its mechanism, its sample sizes and its number of repetitions. Power against a planted defect is not the
probability of detecting a real bank's problem. No size or power figure here is a regulatory threshold, and a test that
holds its size does not make a model compliant.
