\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The cases differ in data and in method, and they share one protocol, so that a light, an interval or a finding means the same thing in every case. This section states it: the questions and the
variants, the splits, the seeds and repetitions, the statistics, the policy for many tests at once, the oracles against which results are judged, and the hardware.

\paragraph{Cases and variants.} The \rsCasesResult{} built cases ask the question stated in the first column of \cref{tab:results-summary}, and each is run on the variants of \cref{tab:data-splits}: \rsVariants{} committed variant documents in all. A variant is a regime
that the case wants to show (a shift of the population, a crisis window, a deficiency planted in a generator, a scenario of the supervisor), and a case without a meaningful parametric or temporal family ships as a single documented benchmark and is not padded. The default variant
is the one the application opens on (\cref{tab:protocol-cases}). Every method of a case is run on the same data and the same split as the others, and a published method is run by the product and never compared through the numbers that its authors reported.

\paragraph{Splits.} The split of each case is fixed before any model is fitted and is stated in \cref{tab:data-splits}. Transforms, binnings and imputations are fitted on the training slice only; a calibration slice is disjoint from the test slice; the shifts of a
robustness variant are applied to the evaluation set only; and the fitted parameters of a case must not change when a value outside its training information set is perturbed, which a test of the repository enforces (\cref{sec:bs3}). Where the data have a time order, the split is out of time, by approval year, default date,
reporting date or rolling window, and the forecast of a day uses data up to the day before.

\paragraph{Seeds and repetitions.} Every bake is seeded, and \rsSeedDefault{} is the seed of \rsSeedDefaultCases{} of the \rsCasesResult{} cases; the seed of each case is in \cref{tab:protocol-cases}. Each repetition of a simulation draws from its own random stream, derived from the seed base and the index of the repetition, so that repetitions are independent and a rate can be recomputed from the configuration. The repetition counts are those of the question: at least \pxOneNullReps{} independent repetitions for each test in the null runs of the validation of the validator and \pxOneLeakReps{} for the leakage plants, \pxThirteenN{} replications for each variant of the desk-level generator of the profit-and-loss attribution, \pxFourteenReps{} for the coverage of the anchored default probability, \pxFifteenReps{} series of \pxFifteenHorizon{} days for the sequential monitors, and \pxTwelveHistories{} histories over the \pxTwelveCells{} cells of the operational-risk grid. The bakes write the same bytes on any device, because the thread counts of the numerical libraries are pinned to four.

\paragraph{Statistics.} A proportion is reported with its Wilson interval, a simulated rate with its Monte Carlo standard error, a difference of two areas under the curve with the DeLong variance for correlated curves, a bias with the standard error of the mean of the repetitions, and a correlation or a coefficient with a profile or a bootstrap interval where the likelihood allows one. The
lights follow \cref{eq:vt-light} with $\alpha_{\mathrm{amber}}=\rsAlphaAmber{}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{red}}=\rsAlphaRed{}$ for a p-value test and the cut points of the policy for a metric test; they are conventions and are shown as such. A test that cannot be evaluated is
reported as not evaluated with its reason and is never coloured, and it is counted apart in \cref{tab:results-lights}.

\paragraph{Many tests at once.} The battery of a case runs many tests, and the report does not hide it: of the \rsTestRows{} test rows written over all variants, \rsPTestRows{} are decided by a p-value, and the default variant of the card case alone has \rsCOnePTests{} of its \rsCOneTests{} rows decided that way. Under a correct model and independent tests, a battery of $m$ tests at level $\alpha$ colours on average $\alpha m$ of them amber or red by chance alone, so a handful of amber lights in a long battery is expected and is not a finding by itself. The product applies no battery-wide correction to the lights, because the lights are a triage for a validator and not a decision on the model. A correction is applied where a single test combines several statistics: the complement to the profit-and-loss attribution test uses a Bonferroni correction across its statistics (\cref{sec:bs13}), and the pairwise comparison of rows of a transition matrix uses a Holm step-down adjustment (\cref{sec:m28}). The size of the tests under their null is measured, so that an excess is visible and not corrected for in silence (\cref{sec:bs1}).

\paragraph{Oracles.} A result is judged against one of six oracles, in decreasing order of strength. A golden value from an official worked example or a published table is the strongest, and a test that cannot reproduce its golden value is not adopted. Known truth from a generator measures estimator error and the size and power of a test, with their Monte Carlo error. Real outcomes on held-out data with a leakage-safe split measure discrimination and calibration, and they are as trustworthy as the split is. Identities and invariants, such as the roll-forward of the provisions, the Euler sums, the row sums of the transition matrices, the sum of the parts of the economic value and the monotonicities, hold to a stated tolerance. An independent reconciliation holds the engine's own implementation to a reference engine, to the standard library of the statistical package, or to a second computation that shares no function with it. Licence lineage refuses an artifact derived from a source whose class forbids it. No oracle is a model judging another model.

\paragraph{Acceptance.} Each method states its acceptance criterion before it runs: the golden value it must reproduce, the interval inside which a known truth must fall, the size it must hold, the power it must reach. A proposed method also states the result that refutes it, fixed before the evaluation, and it is reported whatever its result (\cref{sec:proposed}).

\paragraph{Hardware and run times.} The bakes ran on a Windows workstation with \rsRamGb{} GB of free memory and no graphics processor, with Python \rsPythonDev{} and Node \rsNodeDev{} (\rsPythonCi{} and~\rsNodeCi{} in continuous integration, which runs only the tests and the web build). \Cref{tab:protocol-resources} gives the memory and the time of the fetch and of the bakes that the design measured; the validation of the validator is the longest by far, because it simulates every registered test under its null and under seven families of defects.
