A bank that models its capital by risk type obtains stand-alone loss distributions and must report one aggregate. Every figure
below the simple sum asserts a diversification benefit that rests on a dependence structure the bank cannot observe in the joint
tail. We study a reporting protocol that never shows the aggregate alone: each aggregate is shown with a band of eight rows over
the same marginals, one horizon and one confidence level (a lower bound, the simple sum, the square-root rule, the Gaussian copula,
$t$ copulas with 4, 8 and 30 degrees of freedom and the sharp worst case), only the simple sum is labelled capital, and an
Euler allocation of the aggregate expected shortfall adds up to the aggregate. Nine checks fail when the rule is broken. We prove
what holds for the band and run the protocol with the engine \texttt{riskvalidation} 1.1.0 on the six committed aggregates of a
synthetic bank and on ten planted dependence structures of known capital. The structure holds in all six aggregates, and the band
runs from CLP \bdLower{} to \bdWorst{} billion against a headline of CLP \bdHeadline{} billion, \bdWidthOverSe{} Monte Carlo standard errors
wide. The sharp range contains all ten planted capitals, with a median width of $\bdAblFullWidth$ times the capital. The interior rows
of the base matrix contain $\bdCovInterior$ of the ten, against 75 percent predicted, and $\bdCovInteriorStress$ with the rows of the
market-credit stress. The Gaussian copula alone is within 5 percent for $\bdGaussWithin$ of the ten, and two single-figure estimators
calibrated on the structure itself for $\bdAblHybridPoint$. Two of nine predictions were refuted. The band exposes the dependence
uncertainty; it does not reduce it, and it is conditional on the marginals.
