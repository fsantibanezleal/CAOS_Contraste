Validating a probability-of-default model means reporting its discrimination on data that no fitting step read, yet binning, weights of evidence, feature screens, configuration choices and calibration maps are routinely fitted with the rows that the estimate treats as new. We state a leakage-safe protocol per case: a split declared before any fit, a rule that fixes the training window of a dated case, and three invariance gates that fail when a fitted parameter changes under a perturbation of the rows outside the training slice, with a control against vacuous passes. We prove that the holdout estimate is unbiased when no step read the holdout, give the exact probability that the outside-information gate detects a leak through a quantile edge, and derive the AUC of a score with a leak that exists only in development. On planted data with a known truth, on 30,000 card holders and on the product's own scorecard, the protocol's AUC bias is \nv{syn.w.none.bias}$\,\pm\,$\nv{syn.w.none.ci} (95\% interval) with \nv{syn.w.none.cov}\% coverage of the engine's interval; a weight of evidence computed with holdout labels adds \nv{syn.w.woe.bias} and a fit on every row \nv{syn.w.fit.bias}, with coverage falling to \nv{syn.w.fit.cov}\%. At least one gate fails for each of \nv{h5.total} planted leaks in all \nv{e2.reps} draws and none fails on the protocol. A leak that exists only in development is detected by the AUC against development in \nv{e3.s0.5.auc_test}\% of repetitions at a shift of half a standard deviation and by the stability index of the leaked feature in \nv{e3.s1.0.csi_test}\% at a shift of one. On five cohorts of loans a random split reproduces the level of the 5-year rate within \nv{e4.holdse} points where the declared cohort shows gaps of up to \nv{e4.2006-07.sc.aoot_gap} points. One of nine predictions did not hold.
