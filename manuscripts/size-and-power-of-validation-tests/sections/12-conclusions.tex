\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The size and the power of validation tests can be measured, with their error, on generators whose truth is known, and the measurement changes what the lights of a validation report are taken to mean.
A test holds its size when neither its exact rejection probability nor the Wilson interval of its rate lies above the level at 5\,\% or at 1\,\%.
By that definition, with \nRepsPooled{} repetitions per test, \nHold{} of the \nTests{} tests of the credit families hold their size and \nNotHold{} do not: the Jeffreys test of the default probability has an exact size of \jeffExactOne\,\% at the 1\,\% level, the normal test over five years rejects \pooledNorm\,\% of correct models at 5\,\% (Wilson interval \pooledNormLo\,\% to \pooledNormHi\,\%), and \nSmallOver{} further tests exceed their level by \smallExcMin{} to \smallExcMax{} points.
The committed cells of \nNullReps{} repetitions resolve about \detIntFiveFour{} points and show \nHoldCellDef{} of the \nTests{} tests as holding by the same definition (\nHoldCellRule{} pass the screen, and \nHoldPoolRule{} pass it on the pool), so the judgement of a single cell differs from that of the pool for \nVerdictDisagree{} tests; a statement that a test holds its size is a statement with a resolution.
The largest departures come from assumptions that fail in real portfolios, and they are explained by short derivations: defaults that are correlated give the binomial test a size of \binRhoOneZeroExact\,\% at an asset correlation of 10\,\% in a portfolio of 1,000 and a limit of \binLimitRhoTen\,\% for large portfolios, a degrees-of-freedom convention and a reference value that is an estimate move a size to \hlFitted\,\% and \aucDevOne\,\%, a fixed band of the stability index alarms in \psiHundred\,\% of samples of 100 in which nothing changed, and a bounded statistic gives the concentration test no power at all with ten grades at an initial coefficient of variation of 0.6.
On power, \nReach{} of the \nPowerTests{} tests reach 80\,\% on the ladder of the defect planted for them, and the Vasicek-corrected binomial test, the default-profile test, the characteristic stability index and the concentration test do not.
The instrument is sound where it can be checked: all \nAgree{} exact comparisons agree, the three published simulation studies that can be rerun are rerun with \wpAgree{} of \wpTotal{} and \ynAgree{} of \ynTotal{} cells reproduced, and all \nReproTotal{} committed cells reproduce exactly from their seeds.
Three of the eight predictions are refuted, those on the size of every test, on the stability of a single cell's judgement and on the reach of every power curve, and they are reported as such; the predictions were written after the committed measurement on which six of them are decided, and P4 repeats the counts that the design requirement records as observed, so they are checks of the record and not forecasts.

The measurement has limits that the generators and ladders impose: it describes the tests on the scenarios it draws, it does not describe a bank, and it implies no threshold.
The next steps are to plant defects of the transition process, so that the four transition tests have a power, to extend the generators to serial correlation of the systematic factor and to several factors, to run the extension tests along ladders, and to repeat the measurement on each new release of the engine, since a test that changes changes its size and its power.
